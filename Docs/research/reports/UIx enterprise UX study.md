# Build the layer above UIx's primitives

UIx 2.70.0 (`origin/master` 55d5e1d4) already ships most of what an enterprise-UX checklist would ask for. It has a ranked, diacritic-folding command palette. Its DataWorkspace covers multi-sort, saved views, capped bulk selection, grouping, virtualisation and a keyboard row model. It has a mature `Field` system with error, warning and success states, a labels provider guarded by a literal-string CI gate, and about 120 axe-gated browser specs. So the next gains come from **the layer above those primitives**, not from new widgets. Six kit gaps decide the next two quarters. The first is a shared **shortcut registry**: a `?` sheet, an off switch for character keys (WCAG 2.1.4), and one source that feeds the palette, hints and row verbs. The second is **AI-proposal primitives** built on the review pieces UIx already has (`Input review`, `DiffViewer`, `MatchReview`), with structural approval, never optional review, for consequential ITSM writes. The third is a React **`ErrorSummary`** plus page-level unsaved-change and conflict handling. The fourth is **plural-aware labels** with shipped de, bs, hr, sr-Latn and sr-Cyrl packs, because one/many pairs miscount in the home market. The fifth is **manual assistive-technology evidence** for the complex widgets; only Button has any today. The sixth is a typed, content-free **instrumentation seam**. The competitor teardown says this is where TENSOR can win. No ITSM incumbent documents a global command palette or a background-task tray. Zendesk and ServiceNow make shortcuts a per-user opt-in, and JSM queues cannot resize columns. J/K queue traversal, side-panel bulk edit and AI summaries, on the other hand, are table stakes. ADR-0038 bans outside palette and toast libraries and hand-rolled equivalents, so each of these capabilities lands in UIx or none of the five products gets it. Two clocks set the order. EN 301 549 V4.1.1 (WCAG 2.2 AA) is expected in the EU Official Journal between 16 December 2026 and 22 January 2027. And the products pinned UIx 2.16 to 2.26 when the ADR was accepted, so much of the "missing" UX is an adoption gap that a pin bump closes at no cost to the kit.

## Most of the checklist ships; the gaps sit one layer up

The research notes kept arriving at recommendations UIx has already built. The kit already has a palette with recents, groups and `aria-keyshortcuts` ([CommandPalette.tsx](https://github.com/COx-ORG-H/UIx/blob/master/packages/react/src/components/CommandPalette.tsx)). It has skeleton timing that matches Vercel's guidance: `useDelayedPending` shows nothing for 300 ms and then stays visible for at least 500 ms, against Vercel's 150–300 ms show-delay and 300–500 ms minimum ([useDelayedPending.ts](https://github.com/COx-ORG-H/UIx/blob/master/packages/react/src/hooks/useDelayedPending.ts); [Vercel guidelines](https://vercel.com/design/guidelines)). Optimistic updates are already limited to reversible personal actions ([useOptimisticValue.ts](https://github.com/COx-ORG-H/UIx/blob/master/packages/react/src/hooks/useOptimisticValue.ts)). An undo toast exists ([toast-store.ts](https://github.com/COx-ORG-H/UIx/blob/master/packages/react/src/toast-store.ts)). The table holds a "keep previous rows" `refreshing` state. Fields have a non-blocking `warning` severity ([Field.tsx](https://github.com/COx-ORG-H/UIx/blob/master/packages/react/src/components/Field.tsx)). There is a type-to-confirm dialog, conflict-aware `InlineEdit`, a dirty-dialog guard, a target-size ratchet, a 320 px reflow test, a forced-colours layer, and five display-preference axes, with a sixth in PR #290 ([index.ts](https://github.com/COx-ORG-H/UIx/blob/master/packages/react/src/index.ts); [display-preferences-model.ts](https://github.com/COx-ORG-H/UIx/blob/master/packages/react/src/display-preferences-model.ts)). A study that recommended "add saved views, density, virtualisation, undo and delayed spinners" would be recommending the status quo.

The real gaps share a shape. UIx ships the leaves, such as `Kbd`, `ShortcutHint` and per-component hotkeys, but not the tree that coordinates them. `AppShell` has its own "ignore while typing" guard for `navHotkey`. DataWorkspace exposes `activeRowId` "to drive it from the product's own shortcuts (j / k)", which leaves the keys themselves to each product ([DataWorkspace.tsx](https://github.com/COx-ORG-H/UIx/blob/master/packages/react/src/components/DataWorkspace.tsx)). The validation summary exists only as CSS, `.uix-validation-summary`, and no React component references it ([form.css](https://github.com/COx-ORG-H/UIx/blob/master/packages/tokens/styles/components/form.css)). `UixLabelsProvider` covers about 50 namespaces, but `Intl.PluralRules` appears zero times in `packages/react/src` ([labels-context.tsx](https://github.com/COx-ORG-H/UIx/blob/master/packages/react/src/labels-context.tsx)). The manual screen-reader protocol is written, but its only result file is for Button, recorded at UIx 2.6.0 on 2026-07-03 ([results/button.md](https://github.com/COx-ORG-H/UIx/blob/master/Docs/a11y/results/button.md)). UIx measures its own build in detail and emits nothing at runtime, apart from the `uix-feedback` event that PR #297 proposes.

**Kit gaps and adoption gaps have to be kept apart.** ADR-0038, accepted on 2026-09-30, binds TENSOR, MOTUS, MEDx, POSx and SHOPx staff apps to `@tensor_1/react` and `@tensor_1/tokens` alone. It says a missing component "is built in UIx first", and it bans "toast and command-palette libraries" and hand-rolled equivalents. At the time, MEDx pinned 2.16.0, MOTUS 2.26.0 and TENSOR 2.26.1, while POSx and SHOPx consumed tokens only ([ADR-0038](file:///E:/Development/Docs/adr/0038-uix-exclusive-staff-apps-and-provider-portal-for-every-product.md)). The earlier motion study noted TENSOR at 2.55.x. Current pins were not re-checked for this report. Everything shipped between 2.26 and 2.70 is therefore a kit capability the products may not have yet. That includes keyboard row navigation (HAR-2626), the DataWorkspace gaps release (2.51), `maxSelection`, `ShortcutHint`, peek focus return, the matrix grids and `uix-page-rules`.

## Incumbents leave keyboard speed and background work open

The ITSM leaders have converged on one queue model: rule-defined saved views, J/K-plus-Enter traversal, multi-select bulk edit in a side panel, and SLA breach time as a sortable column. Nobody has a clearly better queue. JSM documents J or ↓ for next, K or ↑ for previous, and O or Enter to open ([Atlassian](https://support.atlassian.com/jira-service-management-cloud/docs/use-keyboard-shortcuts-in-your-queues/)). Freshservice adds `x` to select and Shift+? for the full shortcut panel, and its shortcuts fire only when focus is outside a text field ([Freshservice](https://support.freshservice.com/support/solutions/articles/234433-keyboard-shortcuts-in-freshservice)). That makes **J/K/X/Enter and a `?` sheet table stakes**. TENSOR should match the keys exactly so muscle memory carries over.

Above that floor the incumbents are weak, and in documented ways. Zendesk enables shortcuts **per user only**, with no admin or global setting. Agents report that most legacy shortcuts are unsupported in Agent Workspace, and a Zendesk PM said there were "no plans" for more in the short term ([Zendesk community](https://support.zendesk.com/hc/ja/community/posts/4482937748634-Keyboard-Shortcuts-Agent-Workspace)). ServiceNow hides its shortcuts behind an accessibility preference, `glide.ui.keyboard.shortcuts.enabled`, although its shortcut modal is usefully context-sensitive ([ServiceNow KB1658839](https://support.servicenow.com/kb?id=kb_article_view&sysparm_article=KB1658839)). The teardown found **no incumbent document describing a global command palette for the agent workspace**, and none describing an agent-visible background-job tray for imports, discovery runs or bulk jobs. Both are "not found" rather than "proven absent". ServiceNow's notification panel in particular was not retrieved. Complaints point the same way. ServiceNow's Service Operations Workspace draws freeze and slowness reports ([ServiceNow Community](https://www.servicenow.com/community/incident-management-forum/does-anyone-else-find-service-operations-workspace-slow/m-p/2604329)). JSM is called cluttered, and its queues "can't resize columns" ([PeerSpot](https://origin.peerspot.com/products/jira-service-management-reviews/page-2)).

### A registry turns scattered hotkeys into one contract

The highest-leverage keyboard item is a `ShortcutProvider` / `useShortcut` registry. It should own five things. The first is scope: global, view, or focused component. The second is key type: single key, sequence such as G then I, or modifier. The third is the "not while typing" rule in one place, covering inputs, contenteditable, open menus and listboxes, and IME composition (the IME case is an inference; no source covered it). The fourth is development-time conflict detection. The fifth is the generated `?` sheet. The `Kbd` hints in palette results and `ShortcutHint` bubbles would read from the same table, so the three surfaces cannot drift. Superhuman and Raycast both use the palette to teach shortcuts by showing each command's keys beside it ([Superhuman](https://blog.superhuman.com/how-to-build-a-remarkable-command-palette/); [Raycast](https://manual.raycast.com/action-panel.md)). UIx's `shortcut` field on palette items already renders that way. It just has no shared source.

The registry is also where compliance lives. **WCAG 2.1.4 (Level A) covers any shortcut made only of printable characters, including Shift+/ (`?`) and sequences like G then A.** Such a shortcut must meet one of three conditions: it can be turned off, it can be remapped to include a modifier, or it is active only while its component has focus ([W3C Understanding 2.1.4](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG21/Understanding/character-key-shortcuts.html)). The criterion exists because speech-input software turns dictated words into keystrokes. GitHub's route is a setting that disables character-key shortcuts and keeps the modifier ones ([GitHub Docs](https://docs.github.com/en/get-started/accessibility/keyboard-shortcuts)). UIx should offer all three conditions. Row verbs (A assign, S status, L label, P priority, E resolve) should be scoped to the focused list, which satisfies the third condition by itself. A `characterKeys: on | off` preference, persisted by the consumer the way display preferences are, covers the first. An `overrides` map keyed by command id covers the second. Every single-key verb also needs a modifier path through the palette or the row action menu. The Zendesk lesson becomes a rule: the shortcut map is a versioned contract with a changelog, so a UI migration cannot quietly drop keys.

Multi-select belongs in the registry too, and it needs one documented model. X or Space toggles the active row. Shift+X, Shift+Arrow or Shift+click extends from the anchor. Mod+A selects all visible rows. Esc clears. When a selection exists, verbs act on it; otherwise they act on the active row. That is Linear's documented behaviour ([Linear: Select issues](https://linear.app/docs/select-issues)) and it agrees with the APG grid selection keys ([W3C APG Grid](https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/patterns/grid/)). Focus after a row disappears matters just as much. When a ticket is resolved or assigned out of the view, focus should move to the next row, or to the previous one if it was the last, and never fall to `<body>`. WCAG 2.1.4's motivating harm is exactly a user who loses their place ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG21/Understanding/character-key-shortcuts.html)). `RovingList` already "survives item removal". Whether DataWorkspace's row model does was not verified, so it needs a browser test before it is called done.

### The palette needs async sources and a per-row action panel, not a rewrite

The palette's ranking model is already good: label prefix, then word-start tokens, then contained tokens, then keywords, all with case and diacritics folded ([command-palette-model.ts](https://github.com/COx-ORG-H/UIx/blob/master/packages/react/src/command-palette-model.ts)). It is missing five things. **Async, server-backed sources** are the first, because TENSOR searches thousands of tickets, users and CIs, and cmdk's own README says it performs well only "up to 2,000-3,000 items" before filtering has to move server-side ([cmdk](https://github.com/pacocoursey/cmdk)). Whether items mode already handles loading states and async results was not verified, so the first task is to read the code. Second, a **page stack** in which Esc, or Backspace on an empty query, pops a level, following cmdk's documented pattern for "Change status → status list". Third, a "Title (matched alias)" suffix, as in Superhuman's "Mark Done (Archive)", which explains a match and teaches the canonical wording. Fourth, a `contextBoost` hook that raises commands fitting the current view instead of hiding the rest. Fifth, a **frecency hook** whose store belongs to the consumer, so per-user data never lives in UIx ([Superhuman](https://blog.superhuman.com/how-to-build-a-remarkable-command-palette/); [Raycast manual](https://manual.raycast.com/search-bar)).

The per-row action panel is a separate surface. In Raycast's model, Enter runs the primary action on the focused item and Cmd/Ctrl+K lists every action with its shortcut ([Raycast](https://manual.raycast.com/action-panel.md)). That maps directly onto a TENSOR ticket row and onto UIx's `RowActions`. It needs Superhuman's conflict rule, where the first press keeps the local meaning and a second press escalates to the global palette, so the two Mod+K surfaces can coexist. The same rule applies inside the rich-text editor if it binds Mod+K to insert a link. On opening speed UIx is already right: the design spec makes keyboard-opened overlays and the palette instant (`[data-uix-instant]`). That matches Rauno Freiberg's point that a command menu used hundreds of times a day should not animate ([Rauno Freiberg](https://rauno.me/craft/interaction-design)). The budget to write down for TENSOR is Superhuman's: **under 100 ms from keypress to visual feedback, and under 50 ms for list navigation and palette open** ([Superhuman](https://blog.superhuman.com/superhuman-is-built-for-speed/)). The INP "good" threshold of 200 ms at p75 is the floor ([web.dev INP](https://web.dev/articles/inp)).

Undo needs only a delta. `toast.undoable` exists. What is missing is a visible countdown that **pauses on hover and focus**, so keyboard and screen-reader users can reach Undo within the time limit (WCAG 2.2.1), plus a Mod+Z binding registered through the registry and collapsing when the same action repeats quickly. Confirmation stays reserved for irreversible or wide-blast actions, where `ConfirmDialog` already exists. No fetched source gave an undo-window length, so the duration is a product decision. PR #293's TaskTray is where bulk actions running longer than about 10 seconds report progress and partial failures. It is the "background work" half of the differentiation story, and its spec should add a distinct, attention-drawing "needs approval" state (see the AI section).

### Dense tables need ergonomics and focus safety, not more features

DataWorkspace is UIx's deepest component, so the table recommendations are deliberately narrow. **Column resize is the most visible hole.** UIx has only a CSS grip, `.uix-table__resize`, which its own 2026-07-01 audit marks as "PROPOSED grip — visual affordance" ([table.css](https://github.com/COx-ORG-H/UIx/blob/master/packages/tokens/styles/components/table.css)), and JSM's lack of resize is a named complaint. Three sources give the shape. AG Grid provides keyboard Alt+Left/Right to resize and Shift+Left/Right to move from a focused header ([AG Grid](https://www.ag-grid.com/react-data-grid/keyboard-navigation/)). React Aria persists widths on `onResizeEnd`, not on every pointer move ([React Aria Table](https://react-aria.adobe.com/Table)). And WCAG 2.5.7 requires a single-pointer alternative to dragging, which keyboard support alone does not satisfy ([W3C Understanding 2.5.7](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/dragging-movements.html)). The engine already has `clampWidth` and URL-serialised `ViewState`, so widths can live in saved views.

**Selection scope** is the second gap. PatternFly separates "Select page" from "Select all" across pages, and its selected-count label always covers every page ([PatternFly](https://www.patternfly.org/patterns/bulk-selection/)). For TENSOR, "select all 4,000 matching" has to be a server-side filter predicate, not an id list. The confirmation should restate both the count and the filter. DataWorkspace has `maxSelection` and a bulk bar, but no all-matching mode was seen in the props read. What happens to the selection when the filter changes is unsourced; PatternFly explicitly does not address it. **Focus behind sticky layers** is the third gap, and an accessibility one. WCAG 2.2's 2.4.11 fails a focused row that is entirely hidden by a sticky header or bottom bar. The documented fix, technique C43, is `scroll-padding` equal to the sticky height ([W3C Understanding 2.4.11](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/focus-not-obscured-minimum.html)). A virtualised scroller that calls `scrollToIndex` must apply the same offset in code. PR #292 adds a hard scroll edge under pinned headers. The natural next step is for each sticky layer (table header, `BulkBar`, toast stack, TaskTray) to register its height into one scroll-padding variable. The fourth gap is that `virtualize` is off with grouping and pagination, so large grouped queues are not windowed ([DataWorkspace.tsx](https://github.com/COx-ORG-H/UIx/blob/master/packages/react/src/components/DataWorkspace.tsx)).

Two table questions should stay experiments. **Density** is the first. UIx's rows are 44, 56 and 68 px ([size.json](https://github.com/COx-ORG-H/UIx/blob/master/packages/tokens/tokens/base/size.json)). Carbon's are 24, 32, 40, 48 and 64 px, with 40 as the default ([Carbon](https://carbondesignsystem.com/components/data-table/usage/)). AG Grid's Quartz default is 42 px ([AG Grid](https://www.ag-grid.com/react-data-grid/dom-virtualisation/)). So **UIx's "compact" is Carbon's "large"**. A 32–36 px step for queue scanning is plausible, but the evidence is design-system convention, not a study of agents. Any step below 40 px has to keep 24 px hit areas for row checkboxes and icon buttons (WCAG 2.5.8), and UIx's target-size ratchet can enforce that. The second experiment is **a spreadsheet tier**. Adrian Roselli argues against turning a table into an ARIA grid just to make rows clickable ([Roselli](https://adrianroselli.com/2023/11/dont-turn-a-table-into-an-aria-grid-just-for-a-clickable-row.html)). The APG reserves the grid pattern for editable or interactive cells, with Enter or F2 to edit and Escape to return to navigation ([W3C APG Grid](https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/patterns/grid/)). TENSOR queues should stay semantic tables. A separate grid tier is justified only for CMDB attribute matrices or bulk-edit sheets, and should wait for a concrete consumer. One ITSM-specific gap is cheap: the roadmap lists `sla` among CSS with no React wrapper ([component-roadmap.md](https://github.com/COx-ORG-H/UIx/blob/master/Docs/component-roadmap.md)). An `SlaTimer` with Halo-style multiple thresholds, which recalculates on pause ([Halo](https://www.usehalo.com/product-updates/whats-new-in-haloitsm-2026-second-release)), is a small, central piece for TENSOR.

## AI proposals need structural approval, not better explanations

AI-assist is the one truly greenfield area. UIx has no AI label, streaming support, suggestion chip, citation component or feedback control. A grep for `stream|partial|incomplete|unterminated` over `markdown.ts` and `markdown-model.ts`, behind the `./markdown` entry, returned nothing ([package.json exports](https://github.com/COx-ORG-H/UIx/blob/master/packages/react/package.json)). Under ADR-0038, a product that wants any AI surface is blocked until UIx ships one, so the kit's choices will set the trust model for all five products.

The research changes what "good" means here. **Bansal et al. (CHI 2021) found that explanations did not improve human-AI team accuracy.** They raised acceptance of AI recommendations whether the AI was right or wrong, and did no better than simply showing confidence ([arXiv 2006.14779](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.14779v2)). Microsoft Research's 2025 overreliance framework states that "overreliance mitigations can backfire" and asks for user testing before deployment ([MSR-TR-2025-4](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/?p=1133469)). Microsoft's playbook adds that the mere presence of sources can raise trust in outputs. That point reached the notes through a search summary, not a full read ([MS Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/ai/playbook/technology-guidance/overreliance-on-ai/overreliance-on-ai)). Vendor practice shows how this goes wrong. ServiceNow pre-fills AI resolution notes and labels the review step "Optional" ([ServiceNow docs](https://www.servicenow.com/docs/r/MlbQAgTiiiMOLOw9T36wJg/hSdzyWv_uWsUK4IydM~SUw)). Zendesk sends approved AI replies under the agent's name without telling customers, and lets admins mark actions "pre-approved" so they run automatically ([Zendesk](https://support.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/9945148867866-About-auto-assist)). Apple's HIG is direct: "Never trick someone into thinking they're interacting with or viewing content authored by a human" ([Apple HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/generative-ai)). The conclusion for UIx is that **for consequential writes, safety has to come from the structure of the flow, not from more persuasive UI**. That means an explicit approve control, no pre-fill-and-save path, and an audit entry that names the AI as proposer and a human as approver.

The kit already has the right foundation. `Input review` is literally specified for "a value the reader should confirm, not an error, e.g. one an assistant filled in" ([Input.tsx](https://github.com/COx-ORG-H/UIx/blob/master/packages/react/src/components/Input.tsx)). `DiffViewer` has keyboard-operable accept, keep and mark-pending actions per entry, plus a `provenance` line ([DiffViewer.tsx](https://github.com/COx-ORG-H/UIx/blob/master/packages/react/src/components/DiffViewer.tsx)). `MatchReview` already renders confidence, or "not scored" when there is none. The new primitives should extend these pieces rather than sit beside them:

| Primitive | Builds on | Behaviour (source) |
|---|---|---|

No decorative glow; Carbon reserves AI styling for identifying AI use |

| `AiSuggestion` chip | `Tag`, `Popover`, registry | Value, a "why" popover naming its inputs, keyboard accept and dismiss. Low-confidence cases switch to N-best alternatives instead of a smaller number ([PAIR](https://pair.withgoogle.com/chapter/explainability-trust/)) |
| `ProposalReview` / approval card | `DiffViewer` | Before/after of every field the action changes, affected records, a reversibility line ("Can be undone for 30 s" or "Cannot be undone"), and Approve / Edit / Reject. Required for consequential actions |
| AI actor in `AuditLog` | `AuditLog`, `Avatar` | "Proposed by Assist, approved by Ana". Matches Halo's scoped AI identities ([Halo](https://www.usehalo.com/product-updates/whats-new-in-haloitsm-2026-second-release)) |
| AI output card | `Markdown`, `BusyRegion`, `CopyButton` | Specific status text ("Summarising work notes"), Stop, Retry, Copy, "Post to work notes", quiet thumbs feedback, sources footer ([Apple HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/generative-ai); [ServiceNow](https://www.servicenow.com/docs/r/Mr7qUatbFXax1_h~z60rmA/zxdYU0fVMKmksUyPRFb1lQ)) |
| Shared AI announcer | One pre-existing polite live region | Never streams tokens into a live region. Announces status, then a heartbeat after about 5 s, then the full reply once; secondary chatter can be muted ([Smashing, Bailey, Nov 2026](https://smashingmagazine.com/2026/11/making-immersive-llm-chat-experiences-more-accessible/)). Atlassian's public bug ROVO-978 shows big vendors ship this gap ([Atlassian](https://jira.atlassian.com/browse/ROVO-978)) |

Consequence decides the gate, and TENSOR's actions fall into three bands. **Low** covers category, labels and priority: suggestion chips, with admin-configurable auto-apply. **Medium** covers assignment, duplicate linking and posting an internal work note: one-click accept, an undo toast, and an audit entry. **High** covers resolving or closing incidents, changing CIs, mass updates, customer-visible replies and change approvals: always a `ProposalReview` with explicit approval, never auto-apply, and a time-boxed undo only where the backend can honour it. In the customer reply composer, Intercom's "Add to composer" model is safer than Tab-accepted ghost text, because Tab-accept on long prose invites unreviewed acceptance ([Intercom](https://www.intercom.com/help/en/articles/8587194-how-to-use-fin-ai-copilot)). That is an inference; no study compared the two. Agentic runs that take time belong in the TaskTray with queued / running / needs-approval / done / failed states. That is the point where the motion study's tray and this study's approval model meet.

Each AI primitive should emit `onAccept`, `onDismiss`, `onEdit`, `onRevert`, `onUndo` and `onFeedback` callbacks. Acceptance rate is the best-validated usage metric. Ziegler et al. matched 2,631 survey responses to telemetry and found it the best predictor of perceived productivity, at 27% for Copilot ([arXiv 2205.06537](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.06537v1)). But acceptance alone rewards overreliance, so TENSOR has to pair it with reversion and downstream correctness: reopened incidents, and reassignment after an AI-suggested assignment. Practitioner thresholds such as "reversion above 5% disables automation" are illustrative, not measured ([Smashing, Feb 2026](https://smashingmagazine.com/2026/02/designing-agentic-ai-practical-ux-patterns/)). On differentiation, practitioners report that ServiceNow's agentic features "only pay off if your CMDB is accurate" ([StackScout](https://stackscout.co/blog/servicenow-ai-review-2026/)). TENSOR's CMDB-native data and an honest, disclosed approval trail make a better story than matching Zendesk on autonomous execution.

## Forms fail at the page boundary, not the field

UIx's field-level machinery is mature. The failures cluster where a form meets the page, the router and the server. The only quantified timing study is small but directional. In Wroblewski and Etre's 2009 test (22 participants, six variants), the best inline-validation variant beat submit-only validation with **22% higher success, 22% fewer errors, 31% higher satisfaction and 42% faster completion**. "After" (on-blur) validation was 7–10 seconds faster than validating while typing ([A List Apart](https://alistapart.com/article/inline-validation-in-web-forms/)). GOV.UK takes the opposite default and validates only on submit ([GOV.UK Validation](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/patterns/validation/)), but its context is one-question-per-page citizen services. GOV.UK itself allows grouped questions for internal tools where users repeat tasks quickly ([GOV.UK Question pages](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/patterns/question-pages/)), which describes TENSOR. The synthesis to adopt is "reward early, punish late". Validate touched, non-empty fields on blur. Once a field shows an error, re-validate on every change and clear it the moment it passes. Check empty required fields only on submit. Never disable the submit button to signal that the form is invalid ([Smashing](https://www.smashingmagazine.com/2022/09/inline-validation-web-forms-ux/); [Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/inline-form-validation)).

The submit layer is where UIx is missing a component. **A React `ErrorSummary` is the smallest high-value item in this study, because its CSS already exists.** GOV.UK's spec is precise. Always show a summary, even for a single error. Move keyboard focus to it, which GOV.UK Frontend has done automatically since February 2025. Link each entry to its field, or to the first sub-field of a date group. Prefix the page title with "Error: ". Use the same wording in the summary and in the field message. Put `role="alert"` on a nested element, the change GOV.UK made in v4.4.0 because JAWS was not reading the whole summary ([GOV.UK Error summary](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/components/error-summary/)). Inside UIx's app shell, the focus move also has to scroll within the form's own scroll container, such as a Drawer or a DataWorkspace pane, not the window. One existing behaviour needs a second look. `Field` announces its error with `role="alert"` ([Field.tsx](https://github.com/COx-ORG-H/UIx/blob/master/packages/react/src/components/Field.tsx)). Under blur validation, that interrupts the user on every field they leave. Common WAI practice puts the announcement in the submit-time summary and associates field errors silently through `aria-describedby`, but the notes could not re-verify that practice this session. The fix belongs in the same PR as the summary, with screen-reader testing. The visually hidden "Error:" prefix must be a translatable label, as GOV.UK makes it for Welsh ([GOV.UK Error message](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/components/error-message/)).

The page boundary needs three more pieces. The first is a **route-level unsaved-changes guard**. `DiscardPrompt` covers dirty Drawers and Modals only, and no `beforeunload` or router guard exists ([DiscardPrompt.tsx](https://github.com/COx-ORG-H/UIx/blob/master/packages/react/src/components/DiscardPrompt.tsx)). It should be armed only while the form is dirty, saving or failed, and disarmed once saved, so autosaved forms do not cry wolf. Its dialog should name the fields at risk and make "Stay" the default. The second is a **record-level conflict notice**. When a 409 or 412 arrives, an inline banner (not a toast) says "This incident was changed by Alex at 14:02 while you were editing". It shows a field-level diff for the overlapping fields only, through `DiffViewer`. It offers "Reload and keep my edits", "Review changes" and "Discard mine". Non-overlapping changes merge quietly. The backend consensus is optimistic concurrency with ETags ([SAP Community](https://community.sap.com/t5/technology-blog-posts-by-sap/handling-concurrent-updates-in-cap-java/ba-p/14403125); [Atlassian developer blog](https://www.atlassian.com/blog/developer/reliable-data-storage-using-optimistic-locking-in-forge-sql)). No usability study of conflict dialogs exists in the notes, so the UX is practitioner consensus. `InlineEdit`'s "Reload latest" path proves the per-field half already. The third is **richer `SaveStatus` semantics**: add `dirty` ("Unsaved changes"), `offline` ("Changes kept on this device") and a conflict route to today's idle, saving, saved and failed states, and announce only the transitions to saved and to error ([saasui.design](https://www.saasui.design/blog/saas-autosave-save-states-ux-patterns)). Status changes, approvals and the MEDx and POSx commits should autosave a draft but never commit the record silently.

Server errors need a shared taxonomy that the summary and banners can render. Field validation (400/422) maps onto fields and the summary. Form-level business rules ("change window overlaps a freeze") become summary entries that link to a section. Conflicts open the notice. Expired authentication keeps the draft and re-authenticates in a dialog. Transient failures show "Not submitted. Your answers are kept." with Retry. An unknown outcome says so honestly and checks status before offering a retry. GOV.UK's rule that server validation is always needed and should feed the same channels backs the first two ([GOV.UK Validation](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/patterns/validation/)). The rest are engineering inference. Idempotency keys stay product-owned. The kit's part is an in-flight state that ignores duplicate activations without disabling the button before the first click. Per-item partial-failure results ("1 of 3 tasks failed: Retry failed task") should reuse the TaskTray's failure rows rather than a new component.

Three smaller field rules round this out. Carbon marks whichever of required or optional is rarer, in words, consistently ([Carbon Form](https://carbondesignsystem.com/components/form/usage/)). A `necessity` setting at form level would let TENSOR's mostly optional agent forms show "Required" while POSx and MEDx's short forms show "(optional)". Read-only, not disabled, should be the default for computed or locked values such as ticket number and SLA due, because disabled fields drop out of focus and screen-reader discovery ([Carbon](https://carbondesignsystem.com/components/form/usage/)). `InlineEdit` already has `readOnlyReason`. And WCAG 3.3.8 fails a split one-character-per-box code input if pasting the full code fills only the first box ([W3C Understanding 3.3.8](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/accessible-authentication-minimum.html)). `OtpInput` exists, but its paste behaviour was not verified, so it needs a test now that 3.3.8 is entering EN 301 549. Long TENSOR change requests suit a task-list pattern, with sections marked Not started / In progress / Complete and a check-answers step. The unwrapped `stepper` CSS is the obvious base. Approvers and tasks should be prefilled from the parent incident, which is WCAG 3.3.7's "available to select" ([W3C Understanding 3.3.7](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/redundant-entry.html)).

## One/many plurals miscount in the home market

UIx's translation seam is strong. `UixLabelsProvider` covers about 50 component namespaces, and a CI gate fails new literal strings in `aria-label`, `title`, `placeholder`, `alt` or JSX text ([literal-a11y-text.mjs](https://github.com/COx-ORG-H/UIx/blob/master/packages/react/scripts/literal-a11y-text.mjs)). The weakness is grammatical. Counts are paired keys such as `resultsOne` and `resultsMany`. **CLDR gives Bosnian, Croatian and Serbian three cardinal categories: `one` (1, 21, 31, 101), `few` (2–4, 22–24) and `other` (0, 5–19, 100). English and German have only `one` and `other`** ([Unicode CLDR plural rules](https://www.unicode.org/cldr/charts/latest/supplemental/language_plural_rules.html)). A one/many pair therefore produces the wrong noun form for most counts in the home market, in "{count} selected", group counts, palette result counts and pagination. The fix is a type change. Count-bearing labels should accept either a forms object (`{ one, few, other }`) resolved through a cached `Intl.PluralRules(locale)`, or a function `(n) => string`, while the existing strings keep working as the English fallback. This should come before any label pack ships, because every new count label written today as a one/many pair is future migration debt. Unicode MessageFormat 2 is stable in CLDR 47 ([Unicode blog](https://blog.unicode.org/2025/03/unicode-cldr-47-release-messageformat-2.html)), but `Intl.MessageFormat` is not in browsers. UIx should therefore not take a runtime i18n dependency. Consumers on Lingui, react-intl or i18next pipe their own catalogs through the provider.

With plurals fixed, UIx should ship **separate label packs for `de`, `bs`, `hr`, `sr-Latn` and `sr-Cyrl`**, not one "BCS" catalog. The sr-Cyrl pack can be generated from sr-Latn and then reviewed by a native speaker. Generation needs an exception list, because Gaj's digraphs are not one-to-one: "džep" becomes џеп but "nadživjeti" becomes надживети ([Wikipedia: Gaj's Latin alphabet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaj%27s_Latin_alphabet)). Even large kits skip this market. React Aria lists `hr-HR`, `de-DE` and a legacy `sr-SP` tag, and has no Bosnian ([React Aria](https://react-aria.adobe.com/quality#internationalization)). The provider should normalise to explicit-script tags and never rely on a bare `sr`. Per the notes, CLDR's likely-subtags default for bare `sr` is Cyrillic, which would be a poor default for BiH. That is unverified, but cheap to test. The address form (du or Sie, ti or Vi) is a product-voice decision, which UIx should document per locale rather than choose.

Search and sort need locale-aware helpers. The palette already folds diacritics, so "ubersicht" finds "Übersicht". But "đ" has no Unicode decomposition and survives NFD stripping, and users commonly type it as "dj". A `foldForSearch(str, locale)` helper therefore needs an explicit fold map, and optionally Cyrillic-to-Latin transliteration of the index so Latin queries find Cyrillic data. The notes flag that detail as unverified. `Intl.Collator` appears once in the React source. `table-engine` sorts should use the UI locale with `numeric: true`, because Croatian collation places č and ć after c. MDN recommends `sensitivity: "base"` with `usage: "search"` for matching ([MDN Intl.Collator](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/JavaScript/Reference/Global_Objects/Intl/Collator/Collator)). One helper used by `CommandPalette`, `Combobox`, `SearchSuggest`, `EntityPicker` and DataWorkspace keeps all five consistent.

Layout needs testing more than new code. IBM's figures, via W3C, put translations of strings up to 10 characters at **200–300% of the English length**, falling to about 130% above 70 characters. German compounds such as "Eingabeverarbeitungsfunktionen" will not wrap at all ([W3C: Text size in translation](https://www.w3.org/International/articles/article-text-size)). UIx already uses `hyphens: auto` on page titles, matrix headers and lane names, and bans `break-all` on labels. What it lacks is a **pseudo-locale lane**. FormatJS generates `en-XA` (accented and bracketed), `xx-LS` (padded) and `en-XB` (RTL-wrapped) locales from the same catalog ([FormatJS CLI](https://formatjs.github.io/docs/tooling/cli/)). Rendering the styleguide routes in those locales through the visual-regression project would catch clipped buttons, tabs, segmented controls and headers before real translations arrive. Hyphenation requires a correct `lang` and a browser dictionary ([MDN hyphens](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/hyphens)). Dictionary coverage for bs, hr and sr is unverified, so the lane should include real German and Croatian strings. A Serbian italic check belongs in the same catalogue: render "бгдпт" with `lang="sr"` against `lang="ru"`. Serbian Cyrillic italics need the font's `locl` forms, and coverage is uneven ([TypeDrawers](https://typedrawers.com/discussion/comment/32962)).

RTL is not a current market, and the notes do not justify building it. They do justify a cheap guard. A rough count finds **170 physical-direction declarations against 88 logical ones** in the token styles, including `.uix-validation-summary ul { padding-left }` and the resize grip's `right: -3px`, and there is no RTL test ([form.css](https://github.com/COx-ORG-H/UIx/blob/master/packages/tokens/styles/components/form.css)). A gate in the style of `check-motion`, rejecting new physical properties with existing debt allowlisted, stops the number growing at almost no cost. On writing, the docs Foundations section has pages for tokens, colour, motion, icons and accessibility, but no Writing or Localisation page ([docs.js](https://github.com/COx-ORG-H/UIx/blob/master/packages/tokens/docs/docs.js)). GOV.UK and Atlassian agree on most of the rules that could become defaults. Error copy says what happened and how to fix it, without "please", "sorry", "oops" or "invalid". Empty states are one title, one or two sentences and a single verb CTA. Destructive confirmations name the object, and their primary button repeats the verb ([Atlassian](https://atlassian.design/foundations/content/designing-messages/error-messages); [GOV.UK](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/components/error-message/)). UIx can encode these as defaults: `ConfirmDialog`'s danger variant requires a specific `confirmLabel` and refuses "OK" or "Yes". It can also extend the literal gate into a copy lint for the English banned words. The equivalent German and BCS word lists in the notes are the researcher's proposal and need native review.

## The regulatory clock makes manual AT evidence urgent

**EN 301 549 V4.1.1 was published on 2 September 2026 and brings in the six WCAG 2.2 A/AA criteria: 2.4.11, 2.5.7, 2.5.8, 3.2.6, 3.3.7 and 3.3.8** ([Deque](https://www.deque.com/blog/en-301-549-v4-1-1-is-final-what-changed-what-it-means-and-what-you-should-do/)). It gains presumption of conformity only once it is cited in the Official Journal. Ireland's NDA expects that on 16 December 2026 ([NDA](https://nda.ie/news/en301549-published)). Deque revised its estimate to 22 January 2027. Until then V3.2.1 (WCAG 2.1) stays the operative version ([DWT](https://www.dwt.com/insights/2026/09/european-accessibility-act-ict-standards-update)). The EAA's direct reach into pure B2B SaaS is contested. Karl Groves argues that a consumer-only reading is risky. French law reaches large private organisations, including intranets, and Italy's Legge Stanca requires EN 301 549 from private ICT suppliers to public administrations ([Karl Groves](https://karlgroves.com/the-european-accessibility-act-and-b2b-software-what-internal-platforms-must-comply-with/)). The practical driver for a BiH vendor selling ITSM into the EU is procurement: ACRs or VPATs per product. Bosnia and Herzegovina's own law is not a driver, and no transposition was found. Deque's advice fits UIx exactly: "start with the design system", because target size, drag alternatives and focus visibility are fixed in components ([Deque](https://www.deque.com/blog/en-301-549-v4-1-1-is-final-what-changed-what-it-means-and-what-you-should-do/)). This is a legal reading by non-lawyers, and scope needs counsel review.

UIx's automated layer is ahead of most published kits. **Its weak point is evidence that automation cannot produce.** Deque's vendor-run analysis of more than 2,000 audits found that axe caught 57% of issues by volume, but issues under only 16 of 50 WCAG 2.1 AA criteria ([Deque](https://www.deque.com/blog/automated-testing-study-identifies-57-percent-of-digital-accessibility-issues)). React Aria's team found "varied support across screen readers" for comboboxes and concluded that manual testing is "irreplaceable" ([React Aria](https://react-spectrum.adobe.com/blog/building-a-combobox.html)). The WebAIM 2026 survey should weight the matrix: **JAWS 55% primary and NVDA 32.9%, with JAWS + Chrome at 31.2%, NVDA + Chrome at 17.3% and JAWS + Edge at 17.0%**. VoiceOver + Safari is only 4.3% on desktop, but iOS dominates mobile at 73.3% ([WebAIM #11](https://webaim.org/projects/screenreadersurvey11/)). UIx's protocol already names NVDA + Firefox as Tier 1 for Stable ([manual-sr-protocol.md](https://github.com/COx-ORG-H/UIx/blob/master/Docs/a11y/manual-sr-protocol.md)). The survey suggests adding NVDA + Chrome and JAWS + Chrome or Edge as required pairs for desktop operator components, with VoiceOver iOS and TalkBack for the touch consumers. Six widgets should be tested first: DataWorkspace (row model, sort, selection, virtualised `aria-rowcount`), CommandPalette, Combobox and EntityPicker, DatePicker, DiffViewer, and the Toast and live-region system. Each result should carry an AT × browser × date × pass/known-issue row on the component's docs page, which is the Carbon status-tag model ([Carbon](https://carbondesignsystem.com/components/overview/accessibility-status)). Those rows then feed TENSOR's and MOTUS's ACRs directly.

The new WCAG 2.2 criteria map onto specific UIx work. **2.4.11** is the sticky-layer offset described in the tables section. **2.5.7** needs an audit of every drag affordance for a *single-pointer* path, not just a keyboard one: SortableList, Kanban, DashboardCanvas, ViewMenu column reorder, and future column resize and splitters. `useKeyboardGrab` solves keyboard operation, which the criterion does not count ([W3C 2.5.7](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/dragging-movements.html)). Move-up/down buttons or a "Move to…" menu solve it. **2.5.8** is already ratcheted. Roselli's "adversarial conformance" warning applies here: the spacing exception lets tiny controls pass ([Roselli](https://adrianroselli.com/2023/12/2-5-8-adversarial-conformance.html)). Dense rows should give icon buttons real 24 px hit areas rather than lean on the exception. **3.2.6** fits the InfoTip help slots shipped in 2.25.0. AppShell and page-header help slots should hold a fixed relative order. **3.3.7 and 3.3.8** are covered in the forms section.

Several lanes are missing beyond those criteria. The 106-line forced-colours layer has no screenshot project. Microsoft's guidance is to repair state and focus cues with system colours, because forced-colours mode removes box-shadow, and box-shadow focus rings and background-only selected states disappear with it ([Microsoft Edge blog](https://blogs.windows.com/msedgedev/2020/09/17/styling-for-windows-high-contrast-with-new-standards-for-forced-colors/)). A Playwright `forcedColors: 'active'` project over the interactive specimens would turn that layer from asserted into evidenced. Text spacing (1.4.12) and a browser default font size of 20 and 24 px each deserve a lane. UIx's own text scale stops at 125%, and the EN 301 549 user-preference clauses reward respecting platform settings. A label-in-name check (2.5.3, the visible text contained in the accessible name) belongs in `uix-page-rules`, because voice-control users say "click Assign" ([GOV.UK AT testing](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/technology/testing-with-assistive-technologies)). A quarterly manual run inside an RDP or Citrix session at 150–200% scaling, with a contrast theme on, covers TENSOR's VDI customers. No primary source on VDI passthrough was found, so this is inference. A `SessionTimeout` pattern that warns well before the 20-second minimum WCAG allows (the notes suggest two minutes), offers a one-click extension and keeps drafts (2.2.1), and a toast rate limit or do-not-disturb mode (2.2.4, toast storms during a major incident), complete the cognitive side. WCAG 3 is years away and APCA was pulled from its draft ([Roselli, Apr 2026](https://adrianroselli.com/2026/04/wcag3-contrast-as-of-april-2026.html)), so contrast gates stay on WCAG 2 ratios.

## Measure speed and acceptance without surveilling agents

UIx measures its build with API reports, CSS and chart budgets, a 50,000-row engine guard, visual goldens and a product-side page-rules scanner. It has no runtime seam. The 2026-09 convergence plan deliberately leaves "telemetry adapters in TENSOR" ([convergence plan](https://github.com/COx-ORG-H/UIx/blob/master/Docs/plans/2026-09-08-tensor-uix-component-convergence.md)). That split is correct: **UIx emits, products adapt**. The kit should offer a typed `onUixEvent` provider, the natural generalisation of PR #297's bubbling `uix-feedback` event. It would carry an allow-listed taxonomy: `palette_opened`, `command_run` with `source: palette | shortcut | click | row_menu`, `shortcut_used`, `saved_view_applied`, `bulk_action` with count and scope, `error_summary_shown` with field count, `optimistic_rollback`, `undo_used`, and the AI acceptance callbacks. Events would carry ids, enums, counts and durations, never free text or ticket content. A `data-uix-component` attribute on component roots would let TENSOR's RUM attribute slow INP interactions to UIx components. `web-vitals/attribution` already exposes Long Animation Frame scripts and INP subparts for exactly this ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/find-slow-interactions-in-the-field)). Without a seam, every product re-instruments the palette and the table, and nobody can tell whether the shortcut layer above is used.

Inside the kit, three measurements are missing. The table guard times only the engine, a median sort of 50,000 rows in 250 ms or less ([table-performance.mjs](https://github.com/COx-ORG-H/UIx/blob/master/packages/react/scripts/table-performance.mjs)). It never renders React. A Playwright benchmark of DataWorkspace scroll, select-all and sort at 10,000 rows, under 4× CPU throttling, would catch the regressions that agents feel. The 4× multiplier is a convention, not a validated VDI mapping. The main `@tensor_1/react` entry has no size budget; only the chart entries do. And because GPU-less VDI saturates CPU (Login VSI measured CPU-only Edge rendering peaking at 100% CPU ([Login VSI](https://www.loginvsi.com/resources/blog/gpu-vs-cpu-in-microsoft-edge/))), a nightly low-end lane should run the same scripts with GPU disabled. At portfolio level, the cheapest high-signal report is **version lag plus page-rules counts plus react-scanner usage per product**. Productboard measures adoption from code with react-scanner and lint rules ([Productboard](https://productboard.com/blog/how-we-measure-adoption-of-a-design-system-at-productboard)), and Morningstar cautions that usage counts are not coverage ([Morningstar](https://designsystem.morningstar.com/whats-new/blog/tracking-system-adoption-and-usage)). That report is what separates "UIx lacks X" from "TENSOR pins a version without X".

The product-side programme, which TENSOR owns, should stay small. Use HEART's Goals → Signals → Metrics per area ([Google Research](https://research.google/pubs/measuring-the-user-experience-on-a-large-scale-user-centered-metrics-for-web-applications/)), weighted towards Task success, Happiness and adoption of new shortcuts. For an all-day tool whose use is set by the employer, engagement and retention say little. Use field time-on-task, reported as median and p75, on five to ten core flows. Use Keystroke-Level Model predictions for the highest-frequency flows: KLM predicts expert time within 10–30%, and a composite variant came within 10% on two enterprise web apps with 26 users ([UX Magazine](https://uxmag.com/articles/evaluating-ux-by-measuring-task-efficiency)). Ask SEQ after benchmark tasks (historical mean about 5.5) and run UX-Lite quarterly (median 78.8, against SUS's 68) ([MeasuringU](https://measuringu.com/grading-scales-for-the-ux-lite)). Expert operators are scarce. NN/g puts trustworthy quantitative benchmarks at about 40 participants ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/summary-quant-sample-sizes/)), so small-n within-subject comparisons plus KLM plus telemetry beat a lab benchmark. Privacy sets hard edges. CNIL's February 2026 draft says session replay needs prior, separate consent, with tiered masking ([CNIL draft](https://www.cnil.fr/sites/default/files/2026-02/recommendation_draft_session_replay.pdf)). Employee telemetry carries the power-imbalance problem with consent, so replay stays out of UIx entirely. Reports should aggregate to minimum group sizes so the tool does not become performance surveillance. The legal reading here is the researcher's and needs counsel.

## Prioritised roadmap

Effort is rough and for one engineer: **S** is a week or less, **M** two to four weeks, **L** more than a month or a multi-PR programme. "All" means TENSOR, MOTUS, MEDx, POSx and SHOPx, though POSx and SHOPx gain React-level items only as their staff apps adopt `@tensor_1/react`. Evidence codes: **C** is a controlled study or a large dataset, **N** a normative standard (WCAG, EN 301 549, CLDR), **G** published design-system or platform guidance, **P** practitioner opinion, **V** a vendor doc or claim. Items that change how something looks or moves still go through UIx's recording-and-ask practice and the UI verification bar.

### Tier 4: don't build

The roadmap rules several things out. Outside palette, toast or grid libraries are out, because ADR-0038 bans them and UIx's own pieces already cover the ground. ARIA `grid` on read-mostly queues and `<tr>` click handlers are out (Roselli, APG). Shortcuts off by default, or hidden behind an accessibility preference, are out (the Zendesk and ServiceNow counter-examples). In AI, the list excludes numeric confidence as a default, explanations or source lists presented as trust boosters, any decorative AI glow, any pre-fill-and-save path for consequential writes, auto-apply for customer-visible replies, Tab-accepted ghost text in customer reply composers, and undisclosed AI authorship to requesters. Disabled submit buttons used to signal an invalid form are out. So are infinite scroll on operator queues, a single shared "BCS" catalog, and a runtime i18n-library dependency inside UIx. Measurement must not include any telemetry sink, session replay, or free-text event property in the kit. APCA, or any other non-WCAG-2 contrast metric, must never act as a gate.

### First five

1. **Shortcut registry (#1).** It unblocks five later items (#9, #10, #12, #23, #30) and closes a Level A criterion (2.1.4) that TENSOR's single-key verbs would otherwise fail. It is the clearest whitespace against Zendesk and ServiceNow. ADR-0038 forbids TENSOR from hand-rolling it, so until UIx ships it, every product's j/k layer is either missing or in violation.
2. **`ErrorSummary` (#3).** The CSS exists, the spec is mature (GOV.UK), and it is the only item here that is small, cross-product and evidence-backed at once. It also lays the submit-time foundation that the conflict, autosave and error-taxonomy work (#13) builds on.
3. **Plural-aware labels (#2).** It is a type change that gets more expensive with every new count label, it must land before any label pack, and it fixes wrong grammar for the home market's three languages.
4. **AT evidence for the six complex widgets (#4).** The EN 301 549 citation window opens in about four to nine weeks. Procurement asks for per-product ACRs, and only component-level manual results can support them. Today the kit has one result file.
5. **AI-proposal primitives v1 (#5).** Products cannot ship any AI surface until UIx does, and the first shipped pattern becomes the default trust model. Building it now, on `Input review` and `DiffViewer` with structural approval, sets the rule before a product copies ServiceNow's "Optional" review. Its acceptance and reversion callbacks need the event seam (#6), so #6 starts alongside it.

The pin bump (#8) runs in parallel throughout. It costs the kit nothing and delivers up to 44 minor versions of already-built UX, depending on where each product's pin now sits.

## How strong the evidence is, and what the notes could not verify

The evidence is uneven, and the recommendations lean on it accordingly. **Controlled or large-sample evidence** is limited to a few results. Etre's validation study (n=22, within-subjects, consumer registration forms, 2009) is small and old, with no enterprise replication. Bansal et al. on explanations and Ziegler et al. on acceptance rate (2,631 responses) are peer-reviewed but neither was run in ITSM. KLM accuracy rests on a 26-user validation. The WebAIM survey (1,780 responses) is uncontrolled. Deque's 57% automated-coverage figure comes from its own audits. **Normative sources** are firm: WCAG 2.2, EN 301 549 V4.1.1 and CLDR plural and collation data. So are the requirements built on them (2.1.4, 2.4.11, 2.5.7, 3.3.8, the plural categories). **Published guidance** carries most of the design detail: GOV.UK, Carbon, APG, PAIR, HAX and Apple HIG. It reflects service experience, not experiments. **Practitioner opinion** shapes the conflict, autosave and streaming-accessibility patterns and the agentic metric thresholds: Smashing, NN/g articles without cited studies, Roselli, and Superhuman's blog. **Vendor claims** stay claims: Intercom's "31% efficiency", Freshworks' "up to 80%" and the secondhand 93%/95% Atlassian routing figures. None of them supports any recommendation here.

Several premises remain open. The "no competitor documents a global palette or task tray" finding rests on doc searches. ServiceNow's Horizon site returned 403. SOW list behaviour, the Next Experience notification centre, and any 2026 JSM or ServiceNow palette were not confirmed. TOPdesk, Ivanti and ManageEngine agent UX was not covered from primary sources. Linear's J/K, X and peek behaviour came partly from pages that returned 404. No source gives a sequence-shortcut timeout, an undo-window length or a prefetch hover delay. The EN 301 549 citation date conflicts (16 December 2026 against 22 January 2027). Clause renumbering in V4.1.1, BiH accessibility law, and the final CNIL replay recommendation were not found. The notes did not confirm web-vitals soft-navigation support, the TC39 stage of `Intl.MessageFormat`, browser hyphenation dictionaries for bs, hr and sr, the script of React Aria's Serbian strings, or the `sr` likely-subtag default. Inside UIx, several code reads are still open. Does CommandPalette items mode handle async sources? Do `FilterEditor` or `RuleBuilder` support OR groups? Does `RichTextEditor` have a slash-command menu or bind Mod+K? Does `OtpInput` distribute a pasted code? Do any labels already accept functions? Does DataWorkspace move focus correctly after row removal, and does it have a summary row? Does the ViewMenu column menu offer a non-drag move? The notes also never established whether UIx's font stack covers Serbian `locl` italics. Current consumer pins were not re-checked, and no study anywhere measures validation timing, AI triage or confirm-versus-undo error rates for expert ITSM operators.

## Conclusion

The study's main shift is in where the work is. UIx does not trail the enterprise field on components. Measured against Carbon, Atlassian, Garden and the ITSM vendors' public material, it is ahead on tables, feedback timing and automated accessibility. It trails on **coordination and evidence**. Its shortcuts have no shared contract, its counts have no grammar, its accessibility claims have no manual AT record, its AI story has no approval semantics, and its products cannot tell whether any of it is used. Each of those is a layer over pieces that already exist, which makes the roadmap cheaper than its length suggests. Most Tier 1 items are wiring and contracts, not new visual design.

The competitive implication is sharper than "add AI". The incumbents' documented weaknesses are speed and honesty: opt-in shortcuts, heavy workspaces, optional review of AI output and undisclosed AI authorship. TENSOR can turn UIx's existing discipline (instant keyboard surfaces, review states, a written motion and frequency contract) into a product position. That position is an ITSM console where every action has a key, long work stays visible in a tray, and AI proposes while a named human approves on the record. Because ADR-0038 makes UIx the only legal source of these patterns, getting the contracts right once in the kit is also how MEDx, POSx, MOTUS and SHOPx inherit them without re-deciding.
