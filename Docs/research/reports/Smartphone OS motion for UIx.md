# Borrow phone continuity, skip the glass

UIx's springs and micro-interactions are not where it trails iOS 27, One UI 9 or Android 17. Converted to damping ratios, its four spring tokens already cover Material 3 Expressive's whole range, from the calm "standard" scheme to the bounciest "expressive" token. It already ships interruptible transitions, tooltip morphs, staggered arrival, directional number ticks, a feedback kit and swipe-to-dismiss. What makes the phones feel more refined comes from four mechanisms UIx does not have. The first is **spatial continuity between a list and its detail** (iOS's zoom-from-source, Android's predictive back). The second is **scroll-linked chrome** (iOS scroll-edge effects, One UI 8.5's feathered scroll edges). The third is **velocity-carrying touch gestures with snap points** (sheet detents, rubber-banding). The fourth is **persistent surfaces for ongoing work** (Live Activities, the Now Bar, Live Updates). As of 2026-11-20 the browser platform can carry three of the four without a library: same-document View Transitions have worked in every engine since Firefox 144, and scroll-driven animations since Firefox 159 shipped on 2026-10-27. Velocity hand-off still needs a small JS spring. The headline visual change of the cycle, Liquid Glass, is the one to decline. Apple, Samsung and Google all spent 2025–26 adding opacity, contrast and "reduce blur" controls back in. Refraction works only in Chromium. A blurred fixed bar costs about 12× the GPU time per frame on a mid-range phone. All of that collides with UIx's crash-driven no-animated-filter rule and its VDI blur budget. For TENSOR, a keyboard-first desktop tool, most of the value is in contract hardening, scroll-edge treatment, a transparency preference and a background-task tray. For the touch-leaning apps (POSx, MOTUS, MEDx), it is the shared gesture engine and bottom-sheet detents.

## UIx's springs already span Material's standard-to-expressive range

The team's sense that UIx "lacks refined motion" does not hold up at the level of curves. UIx samples physical springs into CSS `linear()`, pairs each with a settle-time duration, and exposes them as tokens. **`ease-glide`** (stiffness 550, damping 43, 280 ms) has no overshoot. **`ease-pop`** (800/42) overshoots 3%. **`ease-tooltip`** (400/31) overshoots 2%. **`ease-bounce`** (200/16) overshoots 11.6% ([effect.json](https://github.com/COx-ORG-H/UIx/blob/51461e51/packages/tokens/tokens/base/effect.json)). Material 3 Expressive publishes its springs as damping ratio plus stiffness in AOSP source, so the two systems can be compared directly ([AOSP ExpressiveMotionTokens.kt](https://android.googlesource.com/platform/frameworks/support/+/androidx-main/compose/material3/material3/src/commonMain/kotlin/androidx/compose/material3/tokens/ExpressiveMotionTokens.kt); [AOSP StandardMotionTokens.kt](https://android.googlesource.com/platform/frameworks/support/+/androidx-main/compose/material3/material3/src/commonMain/kotlin/androidx/compose/material3/tokens/StandardMotionTokens.kt)).

| Spring | Stiffness | Damping ratio ζ | Peak overshoot | Character |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| UIx `glide` | 550 | ≈0.92 (computed) | ≈0% | M3E *standard* spatial (ζ 0.9) |
| UIx `pop` | 800 | ≈0.74 (computed) | 3% | between M3E expressive Fast (0.6) and Default (0.8) |
| UIx `tooltip` | 400 | ≈0.78 (computed) | 2% | ≈ M3E expressive DefaultSpatial (0.8/380) |
| UIx `bounce` | 200 | ≈0.57 (computed) | 11.6% | bouncier than any M3E token |
| M3E standard DefaultSpatial | 700 | 0.9 | ≈0.15% | "utilitarian UI… recurring interactions" |
| M3E expressive FastSpatial | 800 | 0.6 | ≈9.5% | "hero interactions" |
| M3E effects (all) | 800–3800 | 1.0 | none | colour and opacity only |

The UIx ratios are this report's conversion, ζ = c / 2√(k·m) with mass 1. They are trustworthy because they reproduce UIx's own documented overshoots (3.1%, 2.1% and 11.6%) to the decimal. The M3E overshoot figures are the research notes' calculations, not Google's.

The comparison shows that **UIx already has the full vocabulary. What it lacks is Material's most transferable rule: spatial springs move geometry, effects springs move colour and opacity, and effects never overshoot.** Google's reference puts it explicitly: "For color or alpha animations use the effects equivalent which ensures a 'non-spatial' motion" ([MotionScheme reference](https://developer.android.com/reference/kotlin/androidx/compose/material3/MotionScheme)). Chrome's own spring guidance warns that overshoot on `opacity` produces values outside 0..1 and flickers ([Chrome: Physics-based easing](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/modern-web-guidance/guides/ui-behaviors/physics-based-easing)). UIx's tokens say pop and bounce are "controls only: never on data or status", but nothing in the nine-rule `check-motion` gate stops an overshooting curve from landing on `opacity` or `color` ([check-motion.mjs](https://github.com/COx-ORG-H/UIx/blob/51461e51/packages/tokens/scripts/check-motion.mjs)). That is a cheap, mechanical tenth rule.

The phone vendors' own guidance also validates UIx's contract more than it challenges it. Apple's HIG says to "generally avoid adding motion to UI interactions that occur frequently" and to "let people cancel motion." It also says Liquid Glass motion is emphasised under direct touch but "more subdued" on a trackpad ([HIG Motion](https://developer.apple.com/tutorials/data/design/human-interface-guidelines/motion.json)). Google reserves the expressive scheme for "prominent UI elements and hero interactions" and the standard one for "utilitarian UI elements and recurring interactions" ([MotionScheme reference](https://developer.android.com/reference/kotlin/androidx/compose/material3/MotionScheme)). Google's 46-study research programme also carries a caveat: a "strong minority" preferred calmer designs, and banking-like contexts were singled out as poor fits ([design.google](https://design.google/library/expressive-material-design-google-research)). Samsung's published motion rules cap durations at 100–500 ms and use a single default curve, `[0.22, 0.25, 0.00, 1.00]` ([Samsung Developers: Motion basics](https://developer.samsung.com/one-ui/motion/basic.html)). UIx's frequency budget is the stricter version of the same idea: keyboard or 100+ uses a day means instant, tens a day means ≤150 ms with no travel ([docs.js](https://github.com/COx-ORG-H/UIx/blob/51461e51/packages/tokens/docs/docs.js)). For an ITSM operator console, that is the right setting. Copying M3E's expressive scheme or One UI 7's "more bounce" would move TENSOR in the wrong direction.

One honest limit sits in UIx's spring design. A `linear()` curve carries no velocity, so an interrupted transition restarts its curve from the current value at zero speed ([Chrome: linear()](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/css-ui/css-linear-easing-function)). SwiftUI's `spring` animations, by contrast, replace each other "preserving velocity from one animation to the next" ([Apple docs: Animation](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/animation.md)). OnePlus's "Parallel Processing 2.0" markets the same retargeting idea: new animations "begin before previous actions complete" ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/oxygenos-16-has-new-lock-screen-customization-options-and-a-novel-gemini-integration-184958404.html)). For pointer- and keyboard-driven desktop UI this does not matter, because CSS transitions already retarget from where they are, and UIx's "interruptible" rule mandates transitions over restarting keyframes. It matters only where a finger flings something, which is the gesture gap covered below.

## Four mechanisms the phones have and UIx does not

### Spatial continuity, now cross-engine but in tension with the frequency budget

iOS 26 built its motion language around controls that "morph" rather than appear. Menus "pop open" from their button, action sheets "spring from the tapped source element", and sheets zoom out of the button that summoned them ([WWDC25 219](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/); [WWDC25 356](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/356/); [WWDC25 323](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/323/)). Android's predictive back previews the destination as you drag, scaling the outgoing surface to 90% and fading through at 35% of gesture progress ([Android Developers: predictive back](https://developer.android.com/design/ui/mobile/guides/patterns/predictive-back)).

Half of this is already in UIx. Popovers grow from the trigger through an anchor origin, menus grow from scale(.9), and tooltips travel and morph between neighbours with FLIP scaling ([popover.css](https://github.com/COx-ORG-H/UIx/blob/51461e51/packages/tokens/styles/components/popover.css); [tooltip.css](https://github.com/COx-ORG-H/UIx/blob/51461e51/packages/tokens/styles/components/tooltip.css)). The drawer's touch swipe already lightens its scrim with travel and settles back on cancel ([Drawer.tsx](https://github.com/COx-ORG-H/UIx/blob/51461e51/packages/react/src/components/Drawer.tsx)). What is missing is a morph between two *different* surfaces, such as a table row into the Peek, a dashboard widget into its full view, or a phone-layout list into its detail page. UIx has **zero `startViewTransition` calls**. The View Transitions API was in the 2026-06 plan, but nothing shipped ([uix-styleguide implementation plan](https://github.com/COx-ORG-H/UIx/blob/51461e51/Docs/plans/2026-06-15-uix-styleguide-implementation.md)).

The platform has caught up. **Same-document View Transitions, including `view-transition-class`, became Baseline when Firefox 144 shipped on 2025-10-14** ([web.dev](https://web.dev/blog/same-document-view-transitions-are-now-baseline-newly-available)). Element-scoped transitions, which let two morphs run at once without freezing the page, exist only in Chrome and Edge 147 ([Chrome blog](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/element-scoped-view-transitions)). A document-level transition makes the page non-interactive while it runs, and a new transition skips the running one instead of retargeting it.

That last point collides with TENSOR's reality. An agent opens a ticket from a queue dozens of times an hour, which the frequency budget puts at "≤150 ms, state feedback only, no travel", and keyboard opens must be instant. **A row-to-Peek morph is travel on a high-frequency path, so the contract rules it out for TENSOR's main queue.** The contract-compatible answer to "where did it go?" is cheaper. Open instantly, and when the Peek closes, return focus to the row and mark it with the existing arrival outline ([arrival.css](https://github.com/COx-ORG-H/UIx/blob/51461e51/packages/tokens/styles/components/arrival.css)). The real morph belongs in a `useViewTransition` hook used for occasional transitions: expanding a dashboard widget, the DataWorkspace phone list → detail step, MOTUS and MEDx flows on tablets. The hook should feature-detect, skip under reduced motion or `data-uix-key-opened`, wrap React updates in `flushSync`, and cap durations at about 300 ms.

### Scroll-edge effects, finally Baseline, and a direct fit for dense tables

The least showy idea of the cycle suits TENSOR best. iOS 26's scroll edge effect dissolves content softly as it passes under floating bars. Apple also defines a "hard" style for a toolbar with a pinned accessory such as column headers, and insists that "scroll edge effects are not decorative" ([WWDC25 219](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/); [WWDC25 356](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/356/)). iOS 27 goes further and draws a uniform toolbar once content scrolls beneath it ([MacRumors](https://macrumors.com/2026/06/10/how-liquid-glass-is-changing-in-ios-27)). One UI 8.5 replaced hard scroll cutoffs in menus with soft top and bottom edges ([SamMobile](https://www.sammobile.com/news/one-ui-8-5-samsung-galaxy-s26-ultra-biggest-changes/)).

UIx has nothing here: zero `animation-timeline` hits anywhere in the kit ([uix_current_state grep, packages/tokens/styles](https://github.com/COx-ORG-H/UIx/tree/51461e51/packages/tokens/styles)). **Scroll-driven animations became cross-engine when Firefox 159 enabled them on 2026-10-27; Safari has had them since 26.0** ([MDN Fx159](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Mozilla/Firefox/Releases/159); [WebKit: Interop 2026](https://webkit.org/blog/17818/announcing-interop-2026/)).

The contract-compatible version uses no blur at all. The fade is a static `mask-image` gradient on the scroll container's edges. An `animation-timeline: scroll()` keyframe reveals the sticky header's divider or shadow over the first few pixels of scroll, using only opacity on a pseudo-element; it never animates `box-shadow`, which the `layout` gate rule forbids. The same timeline, as a `view()` timeline on a long incident record's header, can fade in a compact sticky summary (ticket ID, state, assignee) once the big header scrolls away. That is iOS 27's "uniform toolbar" without auto-hiding anything. Browsers without scroll timelines simply show the static divider. Chromium's scroll-state `stuck` query can sharpen the effect but stays an enhancement, since no other engine ships it ([web-features: scroll-state queries](https://web-platform-dx.github.io/web-features-explorer/features/container-scroll-state-queries/)).

### Velocity-carrying gestures and sheet detents, for the touch consumers

Phones' tactile feel lives in gesture physics. iOS 26 sheets rest at partial-height detents, inset with rounded corners, and become opaque and edge-anchored at full height ([WWDC25 323](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/323/)). A fling on Android's predictive back "absorbs the velocity" before committing, and a cancel "swiftly" springs back ([Android Developers: predictive back](https://developer.android.com/design/ui/mobile/guides/patterns/predictive-back)). M3E pairs every commit threshold with feedback: a notification snapping off the stack gives a haptic rumble ([blog.google](https://blog.google/products/android/material-3-expressive-android-wearos-launch/)).

UIx has the start of this. Drawer and toast swipes measure a 6 px slop and a 0.11 px/ms fling velocity over a 100 ms window. The bottom sheet pads for safe areas and is capped at 85dvh ([Drawer.tsx](https://github.com/COx-ORG-H/UIx/blob/51461e51/packages/react/src/components/Drawer.tsx); [drawer.css](https://github.com/COx-ORG-H/UIx/blob/51461e51/packages/tokens/styles/components/drawer.css)). But the constants are duplicated as literals in two components, there are no tokens for resistance or snap points, and the sheet is only open or closed. The release animation, a CSS transition, cannot inherit the finger's speed.

The path is a shared `useSwipeGesture` hook that owns the constants and adds rubber-band resistance past the bounds and a visual threshold cue. That cue is the web stand-in for M3E's haptic, since the Vibration API does nothing on iPhone ([WebKit: An HTML Switch Control](https://webkit.org/blog/15054/an-html-switch-control/)). The hook also needs a `springTo(el, target, { velocity })` helper in `motion.ts` that integrates a damped spring per frame from the release velocity. UIx's policy of shipping "no animation library" survives this. Motion's mini spring would cost only about 4.85 kB ([motion.dev](https://motion.dev/docs/react-reduce-bundle-size); [motion.dev feature comparison](https://motion.dev/docs/feature-comparison)), but the notes could not confirm that it keeps velocity on interruption, and a hand-written integrator of under a hundred lines covers the two or three components that need it. Detents (for example `[0.5, 'full']`) build on top of that. They matter for POSx and MOTUS on tablets and for MEDx front desks, and barely at all for TENSOR's desktop agents.

### Ongoing-activity surfaces: Live Activities as a background-task tray

All the platforms converged on one pattern for long-running work. It is a compact pill that expands on demand, stacks multiple activities, and persists until done. Examples are Samsung's Now Bar, Android 16/17 Live Updates, Xiaomi's Super Island and Nothing's Glyph Progress ([Android Authority: One UI 7](https://www.androidauthority.com/samsung-one-ui-7-beta-hands-on-3506502/); [SamMobile: One UI 8 Live Updates](https://www.sammobile.com/news/one-ui-8-now-bar-integrates-android-16-live-updates/); [Android Authority: Android 17](https://www.androidauthority.com/android-17-stable-rollout-features-3675016/)).

UIx's latency ladder already demands this behaviour: ">10s: determinate progress and a way out" ([docs.js](https://github.com/COx-ORG-H/UIx/blob/51461e51/packages/tokens/docs/docs.js)). But today's answers are one-shot toasts, inline `SaveStatus` and `BusyRegion`. **A persistent task tray for exports, CSV imports, bulk ticket updates and CMDB discovery runs is the most useful non-motion feature on the phones' list for TENSOR.** It is assembled from existing parts: Count, determinate progress, the toast entry and FLIP reflow, and `SuccessState` on completion.

## Every vendor retreated from transparency within a year

Liquid Glass is the cycle's signature change and its clearest cautionary tale. It is a lensing material that "bends, shapes, and concentrates light in real time". Its specular highlights track device motion, and it shifts between light and dark with the content behind it ([WWDC25 219](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/)). Nielsen Norman Group concluded that "Apple is prioritizing spectacle over usability". They cited text over text in Mail, smaller and more crowded targets, and idle motion such as tabs that "bubble and wiggle" ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/liquid-glass/)).

Apple retreated in three steps. iOS 26.1 added a Tinted option because users wanted more opacity ([MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2025/10/20/ios-26-1-liquid-glass-toggle/)). iOS 26.2 added a Lock Screen clock slider ([BGR](https://bgr.com/2017729/ios-26-2-beta-1-liquid-glass-lock-screen-customization)). Then iOS 27 brought a continuous "More Clear" to "More Tinted" slider whose **new Default is more tinted than iOS 26's Clear**, along with stronger diffusion and darkened edges ([MacStories iOS 27 review](https://www.macstories.net/stories/ios-and-ipados-27-review/2/)). Even so, MacStories still finds "severe legibility issues" at the clearest setting wherever content scrolls under floating bars.

Samsung's beta blur made animations "lock up and stutter" ([Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/p-3608214)), and community reports describe live blur being dropped for performance ([Samsung Community](https://r2.community.samsung.com/t5/Questions/Why-are-quick-settings-animations-removed/m-p/22156228)). Android 17 reportedly added a "reduce blur effects" setting, though that report is unverified ([Gadget Hacks](https://android.gadgethacks.com/news/android-17-blurry-aesthetic-explained-beta-changes-and-open-questions/)). Where the vendors did keep blur, they repurposed it as a **legibility backing**, for example behind One UI 8.5's lock-screen notifications ([9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2025/09/25/samsungs-one-ui-8-5-lock-screen-refresh-has-blurred-notifications-new-clock-styles/)).

The web makes the case against glass stronger still. True refraction needs an SVG `feDisplacementMap` inside `backdrop-filter: url()`. Only Chromium accepts that, it is non-standard, and the map must be rebuilt whenever the surface's size changes ([kube.io](https://kube.io/blog/liquid-glass-css-svg/)). Plain backdrop blur raised GPU frame time from about **2 ms to 24 ms** during gentle scrolling on a Pixel 7-class GPU, blowing the 16.7 ms frame budget ([Mozilla bug 1809738](https://bugzilla.mozilla.org/show_bug.cgi?id=1809738)). UIx already learned this the hard way. It allows only a constant 4 px scrim blur because an animated radius "starves software rendering, e.g. VDI desktops without a GPU". The `filter-motion` gate exists because "Linux WebKit crashes on it" ([effect.json](https://github.com/COx-ORG-H/UIx/blob/51461e51/packages/tokens/tokens/base/effect.json); [check-motion.mjs](https://github.com/COx-ORG-H/UIx/blob/51461e51/packages/tokens/scripts/check-motion.mjs)). Apple's "materialize by modulating lensing" and pointer-tracked inner glow are therefore out on two counts: they need animated filters, and they fail the purpose test on a daily-use surface.

What *does* transfer is the accessibility half of the material story. Apple makes glass "frostier" under Reduce Transparency and black-or-white with a border under Increase Contrast ([WWDC25 219](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/)). Samsung pairs "Remove animations" with a "Reduce transparency and blur" switch, though only a community post confirms the label ([Samsung Community](https://r2.community.samsung.com/t5/Tech-Talk/Boost-your-Device/m-p/17945371)). UIx has contrast, motion, text-scale and density axes in `DisplayPreferences`, but **zero handling of `prefers-reduced-transparency`** ([display-preferences-model.ts](https://github.com/COx-ORG-H/UIx/blob/51461e51/packages/react/src/display-preferences-model.ts)). The media query works only in Chromium. Firefox implements it but leaves it off by default, and WebKit declines over privacy concerns ([caniuse](https://caniuse.com/wf-prefers-reduced-transparency)). That is exactly why an in-app `data-transparency="reduce"` axis is needed rather than the media query alone. The axis should set the scrim and spinner blurs to `0px` (the tokens already allow that), raise scrim opacity, and also switch on under `data-contrast="more"`. Apple's continuous slider is the wrong model for UIx, which has no glass worth tuning; a binary axis matches what UIx actually renders.

## Where OS patterns collide with the motion contract

Most of the phones' showier patterns fail one of UIx's written rules. The table gives the collision and the version that survives, so the operator can decide from rules rather than taste.

Two rules in UIx's own docs need tightening before any of this lands. "No bounce in staff UI" is contradicted by the shipped radio dot's 11.6% overshoot. The Properties line still permits "filter / clip-path when needed" in CSS, which the `filter-motion` gate now forbids ([docs.js](https://github.com/COx-ORG-H/UIx/blob/51461e51/packages/tokens/docs/docs.js)). The effective rule is narrower than either sentence. Overshoot is allowed on controls by operator decision, never on data, status or anything that travels, and filters move only from script through `blurIn`.

## Prioritized adoption list

Effort is rough: **S** is two days or less, **M** about a week, **L** two weeks or more. "All" means TENSOR, MOTUS, MEDx, POSx and SHOPx. Every item that changes how motion *feels* still goes through UIx's practice that "motion taste… is shown as a recording or frame pair and asked" ([workspace lesson 12](file:///E:/Development/Docs/lessons/12-ui-design-systems-a11y.md)).

## What the notes could not confirm

Several premises behind the brief are softer than they sound. **No source confirms that One UI 7 animations are "interruptible".** The material says only "non-linear", "bouncy" and "respond better to your finger movements", with a leaker's "parallel" ([Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/samsung-one-ui-7-beta-hands-on-3506502/)). The documented retargeting claim is OnePlus's, and every OEM performance figure (OPPO's 40% tap responsiveness, Xiaomi's 100+ tuned animations) is a vendor number without a baseline. Apple publishes no blur radii, press-scale values or spring parameters for its morphs. The SwiftUI duration/bounce → stiffness/damping formula was recalled from WWDC23 rather than re-fetched, so verify it before any token encodes Apple presets. Whether the iOS 27 tab bar still minimises on scroll, Android 17's "reduce blur" setting, Samsung's "Reduce transparency and blur" label, "Ambient Design" as a One UI 8.5 name and the One UI 9.5 "full Liquid Glass" rumour are all single-source or leak-level. The iOS 26.5 patch that killed the switch-haptic trick comes only from a library README ([ios-haptics README](https://unpkg.com/ios-haptics@3.1.1/README.md)).

On the web side, Firefox's acceptance of `allow-discrete` without transitioning `display` (bug 1882408) can make exit animations vanish, and its current status is unknown. UIx's dialog, popover and toast exits rely on that mechanism, so a Firefox exit check in the motion-policy browser tests is worth adding ([Chrome: animate to/from top layer](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/modern-web-guidance/guides/ui-behaviors/animate-to-from-top-layer)). Firefox `shape()` support and `match-element` in Safari were not confirmed. Scroll-driven animations have been Baseline for only three weeks, so an enterprise fleet on an older Firefox channel will see the static fallback; that is an inference, with no fleet data behind it. Inside UIx, the notes did not read `motion-policy.spec.mjs`, Kanban, DashboardCanvas or SortableList internals, or the TENSOR motion plan. They did not establish which consumers actually run on touch hardware or which UIx version each pins (TENSOR was last noted at 2.55.x). It is also unknown whether `check-motion` parses `animation-timeline` correctly. The global reduced-motion block sets `animation-duration` to .001ms, which may not stop a scroll-linked animation, so Tier 1 item 2 needs its own reduce rule and a browser test.

## Conclusion

The phone OSes did not reveal a better animation curve. They revealed that refinement now comes from *continuity across surfaces* and *honesty about cost*, and both point back to UIx's contract rather than away from it. Apple, Samsung and Google each shipped a maximal look, measured the backlash, and spent the next releases adding opacity, contrast and calmer defaults. UIx already wrote those defaults down as gate rules. So the useful move is to take the mechanisms the phones use to make UI feel spatially continuous: scroll edges, source-anchored morphs on occasional surfaces, gestures that keep their momentum, and surfaces that hold ongoing work. The flourishes stay behind.

For TENSOR in particular, the work that would read as most "polished" to an operator is mostly not animation. It is a table header that clearly shows when content runs beneath it, a record summary that stays in reach, a tray that keeps a 4,000-row import visible, and a ticket queue that stays instant. The real phone-native expression of these ideas belongs in itsmx-mobile. Publishing spring parameters alongside UIx's `linear()` tokens is the cheapest step towards letting that app share one motion vocabulary with the web kit.
