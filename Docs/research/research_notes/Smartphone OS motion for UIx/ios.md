# iOS 26 "Liquid Glass" and iOS 27: UI, motion and micro-interaction inventory (as of 2026-11-20)

Scope: visual design, motion, transitions, micro-interactions in iPhone OS releases iOS 26.x and iOS 27.x, described so a React + CSS design system can reproduce the behavior. Labels: **[iOS 26.0]**, **[26.1]**, **[iOS 27.0]** etc. mark the release that introduced each item. "UNVERIFIED" marks claims not confirmed by a primary or two independent sources.

## 2. Liquid Glass material behavior (refraction, specular, adaptive tint/contrast, variants, later legibility fixes)

### Takeaway
Liquid Glass [iOS 26.0] is a lensing material: it bends and concentrates light rather than scattering it. It adapts its shadow, light/dark appearance and tint to the content beneath. It is meant only for a floating navigation layer and should never stack glass on glass. Point releases added user control (26.1 Clear/Tinted, 26.2 Lock Screen clock slider), and iOS 27 replaced the toggle with a continuous slider and a denser default.

### Cited Findings
- **Lensing** [iOS 26.0]: "Where as previous materials scattered light, this new set of materials dynamically bends, shapes, and concentrates light in real time." — [WWDC25 Meet Liquid Glass (219)](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/)
- **Specular highlights**: highlights follow the geometry. During lock/unlock, "these lights move in space, causing light to travel around the material, defining its silhouette," and in some cases lighting responds to device motion. — [WWDC25 219](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/). MacStories confirms the gyroscope/accelerometer response and 120 Hz real-time rendering. — [MacStories iOS 26 review p.2](https://www.macstories.net/stories/ios-and-ipados-26-the-macstories-review/2/)
- **Adaptive shadow**: shadow opacity rises over text and falls over solid light backgrounds. — [WWDC25 219](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/)
- **Adaptive light/dark**: small elements (symbols, glyphs) flip light/dark based on the content behind them. Large elements (menus, sidebars) adapt but don't flip, because "transitions like these would be distracting." — [WWDC25 219](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/)
- **Tint**: a chosen color generates a range of tones mapped to the brightness of the content underneath, "drawing inspiration from how colored glass works in reality." "Tinting should only be used to bring emphasis to primary elements and actions." — [WWDC25 219](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/). SwiftUI: `.glassEffect(.regular.tint(.green))`, for meaning only. — [WWDC25 323](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/323/)
- **Size-based depth**: larger glass "casts deeper, richer shadows, has more pronounced lensing and refraction effects, and a softer scattering of light." Colorful nearby content can "subtly spill" onto large surfaces such as sidebars. — [WWDC25 219](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/)
- **Variants**: *Regular* has all adaptive behaviors and works over any content. *Clear* is permanently more transparent, has no adaptive behaviors, and needs a dimming layer. Use Clear only over media-rich content, where the content tolerates dimming and the foreground is bold and bright. The two "should never be mixed." — [WWDC25 219](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/)
- **Usage rules**: "best reserved for the navigation layer that floats above the content"; "always avoid glass on glass"; elements on glass use "fills, transparency, and vibrancy"; avoid content/glass intersections in steady states such as first launch. — [WWDC25 219](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/)
- **Grouping/sampling**: glass cannot sample other glass, so nearby glass elements must share a `GlassEffectContainer` to render consistently. — [WWDC25 323](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/323/)
- **Window focus** (iPad/Mac): when a window loses focus, Liquid Glass "visually recedes." In iPadOS 27, inactive apps dim their icons and text automatically. — [WWDC25 219](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/); [WWDC26 269](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2026/269/)
- **[26.1] Clear/Tinted**: a binary choice in Settings > Display & Brightness. "Tinted increases opacity and adds more contrast." It applies system-wide, including apps and Lock Screen notifications. Apple cited summer-beta feedback from users who wanted a more opaque option. It first appeared in 26.1 beta 4 (2025-10-20); 26.1 shipped 2025-11-03. — [MacRumors, 2025-10-20](https://www.macrumors.com/2025/10/20/ios-26-1-liquid-glass-toggle/); [Wikipedia iOS 26](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IOS_26)
- **[26.2] Lock Screen clock**: a slider sets the clock's glass transparency, with a button to make it solid (shipped 2025-12-12). — [BGR, 2025-11-05](https://bgr.com/2017729/ios-26-2-beta-1-liquid-glass-lock-screen-customization); [Wikipedia iOS 26](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IOS_26)
- **[26.5]** "Pride Luminance" wallpaper that refracts light on interaction. **[26.5.1]** fixed a home-screen background desaturation bug and smoothed ProMotion scrolling. — [Wikipedia iOS 26](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IOS_26)
- **[iOS 27.0]** continuous slider, stronger diffusion/blur, darkened edges, brighter specular highlights (see §1). — [MacRumors](https://macrumors.com/2026/06/10/how-liquid-glass-is-changing-in-ios-27); [MacStories p.2](https://www.macstories.net/stories/ios-and-ipados-27-review/2/)
- **Scroll edge effect** [iOS 26.0]: as content scrolls under glass, the soft style "gently dissolves the content into the background." Over dark content it "intelligently switches to apply a subtle dimming instead." The hard style is uniform across the toolbar plus a pinned accessory (e.g., column headers). Use one style and one effect per view, and none where nothing floats. "Scroll edge effects are not decorative." — [WWDC25 219](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/); [WWDC25 356](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/356/). API: `.scrollEdgeEffectStyle(.hard, for: .top)`. — [WWDC25 323](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/323/)
- **Background extension** [iOS 26.0]: `backgroundExtensionEffect()` mirrors and blurs an image outside the safe area so artwork extends behind floating sidebars. — [WWDC25 323](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/323/)

### Inferences
- A web approximation needs four parts: (a) `backdrop-filter: blur() saturate()` for diffusion; (b) an SVG `feDisplacementMap` or a shader for lensing at the edges (costly, so reserve it for small or hero elements); (c) an inner highlight gradient, plus an iOS-27-style darkened edge (1px inner dark ring plus outer shadow); (d) a shadow whose opacity scales with how busy the content underneath is. On the web, (d) is usually a static token per context, since sampling the backdrop isn't practical.
- For enterprise SaaS, the transferable rules matter more than the optics: glass only on floating chrome (toolbars, command bars, popovers), never on table content; no glass on glass; tint reserved for the primary action; an opaque fallback tier; and a scroll-edge fade/dim under sticky headers in place of hard dividers.

### Gaps
- Apple publishes no numeric blur radius, refraction strength, or opacity values for Regular, Clear, Tinted, or the iOS 27 slider steps.
- No primary source on how many discrete steps the iOS 27 slider has.

## 3. Morphing and continuity (button → menu/sheet, tab bar minimize, search, toolbar grouping, sheets/detents, zoom transitions)

### Takeaway
iOS 26's core motion idea is a "singular floating plane." Controls on it morph rather than appear: menus pop open from their button, confirmation dialogs and action sheets spring from their source, sheets can zoom out of a button, glass elements merge and split like liquid, and the tab bar collapses on scroll. iOS 27 kept this, smoothed performance, and reversed some placements (search back inside the tab bar).

### Cited Findings
- Controls "morph between the controls in each context," maintaining "a singular floating plane that the controls live on." — [WWDC25 219](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/)
- **Menus** [iOS 26.0]: "When showing a menu, the bubble simply pops open to reveal the content contained within," keeping the content in place relative to its button. — [WWDC25 219](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/)
- **Materialize/dematerialize**: "Instead of fading, Liquid Glass objects materialize in and out by gradually modulating the light bending and lensing." — [WWDC25 219](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/)
- **Action sheets** [iOS 26.0] no longer rise from the bottom of the screen; they "spring from the tapped source element." Menus, alerts and popovers "flow out of" Liquid Glass controls, and confirmation dialogs morph out of their buttons. — [WWDC25 356](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/356/); [WWDC25 323](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/323/)
- **Zoom sheet transition**: a sheet morphs out of its presenting button via `matchedTransitionSource(id:in:)` on the button and `.navigationTransition(.zoom(sourceID:in:))` on the sheet. — [WWDC25 323](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/323/)
- **[iOS 27.0]** new `CrossFadeNavigationTransition`, a built-in cross-fade navigation transition that needs no source. — [DEV Community WWDC26 SwiftUI breakdown](https://dev.to/arshtechpro/wwdc26-whats-new-in-swiftui-a-developers-breakdown-1333) (via search summary; secondary, API name UNVERIFIED against Apple docs)
- **Glass morph groups**: with `glassEffectID(_:in:)` inside a `GlassEffectContainer`, badges "fluidly morph out of" a toggle when expanded and are "re-absorbed into it" when collapsed. — [WWDC25 323](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/323/)
- **Sheets/detents** [iOS 26.0]: partial-height sheets are inset with a Liquid Glass background. At small heights the bottom edges pull in and nest inside the display's curved corners. At full height the glass "gradually becomes opaque and anchors to the screen edge." Apple recommends removing custom `presentationBackground`. — [WWDC25 323](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/323/). Dragging a sheet upward makes the glass more opaque and grow slightly "to signal deeper engagement." — [WWDC25 356](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/356/)
- **Modal vs parallel**: modal tasks pair glass with a dimming layer; parallel tasks use glass without one. — [WWDC25 356](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/356/)
- **Tab bar minimize** [iOS 26.0]: `.tabBarMinimizeBehavior(.onScrollDown)` collapses the floating tab bar on scroll down and re-expands it on scroll up. `tabViewBottomAccessory` (e.g., a mini player) expands into the freed space and switches to a compact `.inline` layout. — [WWDC25 323](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/323/)
- **[iOS 27.0]** `toolbarMinimizeBehavior(.onScrollDown, for: .navigationBar)`: "The system automatically moves the navigation bar out of the way when I scroll." — [WWDC26 269](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2026/269/)
- **Search** [iOS 26.0]: on iPhone the search field sits at the bottom. It may minimize into a toolbar button that expands to a full-width field above the keyboard (`searchToolbarBehavior(.minimize)`). A `Tab(role: .search)` replaces the tab bar with the search field when selected. — [WWDC25 323](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/323/)
- **[iOS 27.0]** the search button returned inside the tab bar (see §1). A new `Tab(role: .prominent)` places a distinct tab at the bottom trailing edge (the session's example is a shopping cart). — [MacStories p.2](https://www.macstories.net/stories/ios-and-ipados-27-review/2/); [WWDC26 269](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2026/269/)
- **Toolbar grouping** [iOS 26.0]: items grouped through the API share a glass background. `ToolbarSpacer(.fixed)` splits groups; `ToolbarSpacer(.flexible)` expands. Icons are monochrome by default; the primary action (Done) is separate and tinted (blue checkmark). Don't group symbols with text buttons. — [WWDC25 323](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/323/); [WWDC25 356](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/356/)
- **[iOS 27.0] Toolbar overflow**: `visibilityPriority(.high)` keeps items out of overflow when space is limited; `ToolbarOverflowMenu` forces items into overflow; `topBarPinnedTrailing` pins an item (e.g., Share). — [WWDC26 269](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2026/269/)
- **Concentricity** [iOS 26.0]: three shape types. Fixed radius; capsule (radius = height/2); concentric (radius = parent radius − padding). Avoid "pinched" or "flared" corners. Capsules are used for sliders, switches, bars and buttons in touch layouts. — [WWDC25 356](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/356/). API: `.rect(corner: .containerConcentric)`. — [WWDC25 323](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/323/)
- **[iOS 27.0] Reorder**: `.reorderable()` plus `reorderContainer(for:)` work on any container (List, LazyVGrid). The system provides the drag preview, insertion placeholder and drop animation. `swipeActionsContainer()` extends swipe actions to any scroll view. — [WWDC26 269](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2026/269/)
- **[iOS 27.0] Siri orb**: a floating dark Liquid Glass orb "smoothly descends from the Dynamic Island," pulses while listening, shrinks into a "thinking" state, then expands into a translucent overlay. On iPad it emerges from the bezel like a liquid drop and refracts the content beneath. — [MacStories iOS 27 review p.5](https://www.macstories.net/stories/ios-and-ipados-27-review/5/)
- Liquid Glass "fluidly morphs and animates on screen even better than last year" in iOS 27, which the reviewer credits to performance and rendering work. — [MacStories p.2](https://www.macstories.net/stories/ios-and-ipados-27-review/2/)

### Inferences
- Web equivalents: the View Transitions API (`view-transition-name` on trigger and popover/sheet) reproduces "menu pops out of its button" and "sheet zooms from source." For a CSS-only path, set `transform-origin` at the trigger's position and animate scale plus border-radius from the trigger's capsule to the panel's radius. The blob-merge effect in `glassEffectID` can be approximated with the SVG "gooey" filter (blur + alpha threshold) for small clusters only.
- Sheet behavior that transfers to web drawers: inset with rounded corners at partial height; edge-anchored and opaque at full height; opacity increasing with drag progress; a dim scrim only for modal use.
- iOS 27's reversals (search back in the tab bar, a persistent bar per pre-release reporting) argue against auto-hiding primary navigation in enterprise apps. Hide-on-scroll should be opt-in per surface.

### Gaps
- Apple does not publish the spring parameters behind menu/sheet/zoom morphs.
- Whether the iOS 26 tab bar's minimize behavior shipped unchanged in iOS 27 is unverified (see §1).

## 4. Spring physics (SwiftUI model, presets, interruptibility, velocity hand-off; iOS 26/27 changes)

### Takeaway
SwiftUI springs use a duration + bounce model (since iOS 17), with presets `.smooth` (no bounce), `.snappy` (small bounce) and `.bouncy` (more bounce). `spring` animations retarget mid-flight and preserve velocity, and `interpolatingSpring` adds overlapping animations. I found no documented change to the spring model in iOS 26 or 27.

### Cited Findings
- Presets: `smooth` is "a smooth spring animation with a predefined duration and no bounce"; `snappy` has a "small amount of bounce that feels more snappy"; `bouncy` has a "higher amount of bounce." Each has a tunable form `(duration:extraBounce:)`. — [Apple docs: Animation](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/animation.md)
- **Interruptibility/velocity**: `spring` is "a persistent spring animation. When mixed with other spring() or interactiveSpring() animations on the same property, each animation will be replaced by their successor, preserving velocity from one animation to the next," and can blend duration values between springs over `blendDuration`. — [Apple docs: Animation](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/animation.md)
- `interpolatingSpring` "preserves velocity across overlapping animations by adding the effects of each animation"; it also accepts `initialVelocity` and mass/stiffness/damping. — [Apple docs: Animation](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/animation.md)
- `interactiveSpring` is "a spring animation with a lower duration value, intended for driving interactive animations" (i.e., finger-tracking). — [Apple docs: Animation](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/animation.md)
- System overlay timing tokens exist: `systemOverlayAppearance`, `systemOverlayDisappearance`, `systemOverlayDelayedDisappearance`, `systemOverlayDisappearanceDelay`. The docs list them without values or version notes. — [Apple docs: Animation](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/animation.md)
- Example values from Apple: `Spring(duration: 0.5, bounce: 0.3)` in the Spring docs. WWDC23 demos used `bounce: 0.15` as "small bounce" and `0.3` as noticeably bouncy. Pre-iOS 17 defaults were response-based (`response: 0.55, dampingFraction: 0.825`). — search summary of [Apple Spring docs](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/spring.md), [WWDC23 Animate with springs](https://developer.apple.com/la/videos/play/wwdc2023/10158/), [WWDC Notes](https://wwdcnotes.com/notes/wwdc23/10158) (secondary aggregation)
- Liquid Glass's own feel: "an inherent gel-like flexibility" that moves in tandem with input. Interactive glass "scales, bounces, and shimmers" on touch (`.glassEffect(.regular.interactive())`). — [WWDC25 219](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/); [WWDC25 323](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/323/)
- HIG: Liquid Glass motion "responds to direct touch interaction with greater emphasis to reinforce the feeling of a tactile experience, but produces a more subdued effect when a person interacts using a trackpad." — [HIG Motion (JSON)](https://developer.apple.com/tutorials/data/design/human-interface-guidelines/motion.json)

### Inferences
- Web mapping: a duration/bounce spring can be compiled to CSS `linear()` easing for fire-and-forget transitions. True velocity hand-off on interruption needs a JS spring (e.g., Motion/Framer Motion `type: "spring", bounce, visualDuration`), because CSS transitions restart from the current value with zero velocity.
- A pragmatic token set is three presets: smooth (bounce 0), snappy (~0.15), bouncy (~0.3), with ~0.5 s perceptual duration as the documented example baseline. Use bounce only for direct-manipulation feedback; that matches the HIG's lower emphasis for pointer input.

### Gaps
- The exact default durations of `.smooth`/`.snappy`/`.bouncy`, and the bare `.spring` default parameters, were not confirmed from a fetched primary page (the doc pages rendered titles only). Commonly cited values (0.5 s; extraBounce 0) are UNVERIFIED here.
- No source documents rubber-banding constants or iOS 26/27 changes to scroll physics. The 26.5.1 "smooths ProMotion scrolling" fix ([Wikipedia iOS 26](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IOS_26)) gives no detail.

## 5. Micro-interactions (press/lift, toggles/sliders, haptics, symbols, numeric text, Dynamic Island)

### Takeaway
iOS 26's signature micro-interaction is press-to-lift: a quiet control rises into glass on touch, glows from within, and flexes with the finger (switch knobs, slider thumbs, the tab-selection bubble). SF Symbols 7 [iOS 26] added Draw On/Off, Variable Draw and draw-aware Magic Replace, and SF Symbols 8 [iOS 27] appears to carry these forward.

### Cited Findings
- **Press response**: Liquid Glass "responds to interaction by instantly flexing and energizing with light"; "the material illuminates from within," and the glow spreads outward onto nearby glass. Elements can "lift up into Liquid Glass temporarily" so the resting state stays quiet. — [WWDC25 219](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/)
- **Toggles, segmented pickers, sliders** "transform into glass during interaction." — [WWDC25 323](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/323/). Observed: pressing a Settings switch knob enlarges it into a glass form that refracts light as it moves ([ITWorld Korea](https://www.itworld.co.kr/article/4049352/)). The UISwitch glass highlight follows the finger when dragged and can extend past a form sheet's edge ([Apple Developer Forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/848646); user report).
- **Tab-selection bubble**: swiping across the tab bar shows a "selection bubble" that distorts the icons beneath it, with chromatic fringing at its edges. MacStories called it "one of the coolest pieces of motion design I've ever seen on a phone." The text-selection loupe refracts text the same way. — [MacStories iOS 26 review p.2](https://www.macstories.net/stories/ios-and-ipados-26-the-macstories-review/2/)
- **Sliders** [iOS 26.0]: tick marks appear automatically when `step` is set; `neutralValue` sets where the fill starts (e.g., a bidirectional playback-speed slider). — [WWDC25 323](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/323/)
- **Buttons** [iOS 26.0]: capsule by default; `.buttonStyle(.glass)` / `.glassProminent`. — [WWDC25 323](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/323/)
- **[26.1]** slide-to-stop control for alarms and timers. — [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2025/10/20/ios-26-1-liquid-glass-toggle/)
- **SF Symbols 7** [iOS 26.0]: Draw On / Draw Off presets follow each symbol's path direction, with playback By Layer (default, staggered), Whole Symbol (fastest) or Individually (new, sequential, "more intentional"). Draw Off can reverse. Arrowheads travel with their line. **Variable Draw** renders a path to a percentage over a reduced-opacity copy (download progress, temperature). **Magic Replace** preserves a shared enclosure and combines Draw Off (outgoing) with Draw On (incoming). Gradients come from a single color. — [WWDC25 SF Symbols 7 (337)](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/337/)
- **SF Symbols 8** [iOS 27]: more than 7,000 symbols. No primary list of new animation presets was found; secondary reports say last year's animations and variable rendering carry over. — [Apple WWDC26 Design guide](https://developer.apple.com/wwdc26/guides/design/); [IT之家](https://www.ithome.com/0/963/790.htm) (via search summary)
- **Numeric text**: `ContentTransition.numericText(countsDown:)` gives "a nonstandard transition tailored to numeric characters that count up or down" (available since iOS 16; not new). — [Apple docs](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/contenttransition/numerictext(countsdown:).md)
- **Haptics**: `SensoryFeedback` semantic types: start/stop, alignment, increase/decrease, levelChange, selection, pathComplete, success/warning/error, impact(weight|flexibility, intensity). There are also `press(_:)`, `release(_:)` and `selection(_:)` with PressFeedback/ReleaseFeedback/SelectionFeedback for touch-down and touch-up on controls. — [Apple docs: SensoryFeedback](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/sensoryfeedback.md)
- HIG: "Aim for brevity and precision in feedback animations"; "generally avoid adding motion to UI interactions that occur frequently"; "Let people cancel motion." — [HIG Motion](https://developer.apple.com/tutorials/data/design/human-interface-guidelines/motion.json)

### Inferences
- Web press/lift pattern: on `:active` / `pointerdown`, scale the control 1.0 → ~1.05–1.15 (switch thumbs grow more), raise the shadow, and add an inner radial highlight positioned at the pointer (CSS custom properties updated on `pointermove`). Release with a snappy spring. Keep the resting state flat/quiet. On mouse, damp the effect, per the HIG's trackpad guidance.
- Draw On/Off maps to SVG `stroke-dasharray`/`stroke-dashoffset`. Variable Draw maps to a partially drawn stroke over a 30–40%-opacity base stroke. numericText maps to per-digit vertical roll (e.g., `@property`-animated counters or per-digit spans).
- Haptics don't transfer to desktop web. The semantic taxonomy (success/warning/error/selection/increase/decrease) is still useful for naming feedback events and their visual or audio substitutes.

### Gaps
- Availability of `SensoryFeedback.press/release/selection(_:)` (likely iOS 26 or 27) was not confirmed; the doc page doesn't state per-member versions.
- No primary source details Live Activities or Dynamic Island transition changes in iOS 26/27, beyond the iOS 27 Siri orb.
- No Apple numbers (scale factor, duration) for press-lift.
- The NN/g report (§7) says Home Screen page dots morph into a Search pill after a few seconds; no Apple source describes that behavior.

## 6. Accessibility rules for motion and materials (Reduce Motion, Reduce Transparency, Increase Contrast)

### Takeaway
Liquid Glass adapts automatically to accessibility settings. Reduce Transparency makes it frostier, Increase Contrast makes elements mostly black or white with a contrasting border, and Reduce Motion lowers effect intensity and disables the material's elastic behavior. The HIG frames motion as optional and never the only channel for information.

### Cited Findings
- Reduce Transparency makes glass "frostier and obscures more of the content behind it." Increase Contrast makes elements "predominantly black or white and highlights them with a contrasting border." Reduce Motion "decreases the intensity of some effects and disables any elastic properties for the material." These apply automatically. — [WWDC25 219](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/)
- iOS 27 Liquid Glass "adapts to Reduce Transparency and Increase Contrast." — [MacRumors](https://macrumors.com/2026/06/10/how-liquid-glass-is-changing-in-ios-27); [Cult of Mac search summary](https://www.cultofmac.com/news/liquid-glass-changes-ios-27-macos-27)
- System colors were adjusted across Light, Dark and Increased Contrast to work with Liquid Glass and improve hue differentiation. — [WWDC25 356](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/356/)
- HIG: "Make motion optional... avoid using it as the only way to communicate important information"; supplement with haptics and audio. "Gratuitous or excessive animation can distract people and may make them feel disconnected or physically uncomfortable." System components "might also adjust their motion in response to... accessibility settings or different input methods." HIG Motion change log: "Added guidance for Liquid Glass" on 2025-09-09. — [HIG Motion](https://developer.apple.com/tutorials/data/design/human-interface-guidelines/motion.json)
- The user-facing tiers are Clear/Tinted [26.1] and then the continuous slider [27.0], separate from the Reduce Transparency accessibility switch. — [MacRumors 26.1](https://www.macrumors.com/2025/10/20/ios-26-1-liquid-glass-toggle/); [MacStories iOS 27 p.2](https://www.macstories.net/stories/ios-and-ipados-27-review/2/)

### Inferences
- Web mapping: `prefers-reduced-transparency: reduce` → frosted, near-opaque surface (raise background alpha, keep blur). `prefers-contrast: more` → solid black/white fills with a 1–2px contrasting border. `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` → keep state changes, but swap morph/zoom/spring for short opacity crossfades and set bounce to 0 (Apple's "disables elastic properties"). A separate in-app "glass intensity" preference mirrors Apple's slider.

### Gaps
- The fetched HIG Motion page has no explicit Reduce Motion substitution list (e.g., "use crossfade instead of zoom"). The HIG Accessibility page wasn't fetched. A crossfade substitute is implied by the iOS 27 `CrossFadeNavigationTransition` but not documented as the Reduce Motion fallback.

## 7. Criticism and reception: what worked, what failed, what Apple rolled back

### Takeaway
iOS 26 Liquid Glass drew sustained criticism for legibility (text over images and text over text), smaller and crowded targets, unpredictable controls, and gratuitous motion; NN/g said Apple was "prioritizing spectacle over usability." Apple walked it back in steps: Clear/Tinted in 26.1, the Lock Screen clock slider in 26.2, then a denser default, stronger blur/edges, a continuous slider, and search returned to the tab bar in iOS 27. Reviewers broadly consider iOS 27 the fix, with caveats.

### Cited Findings
- NN/g (Raluca Budiu, 2025-10-10), "Liquid Glass Is Cracked, and Usability Suffers in iOS 26": photo chat backgrounds hurt text legibility in Messages; Mail's search bar over previews creates "text on top of text"; Maps icons blend into food photos. App Store search is split into a separate pill that crowds the tab icons. Tap targets are smaller and closer than in iOS 18. Distracting motion includes Home Screen dots morphing into Search, tabs that "bubble and wiggle," pulsing Contacts buttons, and a shimmering, jumping Music title. Safari's forward button appears and disappears; the back-button breadcrumb label is gone. Verdict: "Apple is prioritizing spectacle over usability." — [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/liquid-glass/)
- MacStories (iOS 26) praised the tab-bar motion but noted refraction distorting text, pink-on-red Music tab accents, and notifications that are hard to read over busy wallpapers. — [MacStories iOS 26 review p.2](https://www.macstories.net/stories/ios-and-ipados-26-the-macstories-review/2/)
- Users reported lag, blur and usability complaints after launch. — [MacRumors, 2025-09-17](https://www.macrumors.com/2025/09/17/ios-26-liquid-glass-critiques/); [TechSpot](https://www.techspot.com/news/109517-performance-design-complaints-mount-after-ios-26-liquid.html) (via search summary)
- Rollback 1 [26.1]: the Tinted option, explicitly because of user feedback wanting more opacity. — [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2025/10/20/ios-26-1-liquid-glass-toggle/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/20/apple-will-let-users-roll-back-the-liquid-glass-look-with-new-tinted-option)
- Rollback 2 [27.0]: a more tinted default, stronger diffusion, darkened edges, the slider, search back in the tab bar, glassier context menus. Apple said it "considered feedback deeply as we refined the new design over the past year" (press-quoted). — [MacStories p.2](https://www.macstories.net/stories/ios-and-ipados-27-review/2/); search summary of [BGR](https://bgr.com/2191219/ios-27-liquid-glass-fix-customization/) / [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/06/15/ios-27-adds-new-liquid-glass-slider-on-iphone-heres-what-it-lets-you-do/)
- iOS 27 reception: the Music Home tab is much more readable even at the clearest setting. At the clearest setting you can still live with "severe legibility issues in apps that scroll content under tab bars or floating toolbars." The reviewer worries that customization partly sweeps legibility under the rug, and wants per-app intensity. — [MacStories p.2](https://www.macstories.net/stories/ios-and-ipados-27-review/2/). 9to5Mac says the legibility problems are "largely fixed." — [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/06/12/ios-27-fixes-liquid-glass-and-not-just-with-a-slider/)
- Gurman (pre-release) called iOS 27 Liquid Glass changes "tweaks" rather than anything major. — search summary of [Macworld](https://www.macworld.com/article/3089789/big-changes-are-coming-to-ios-27-but-liquid-glass-is-here-to-stay.html)

### Inferences
- Lessons for an enterprise web kit: (1) never put translucent chrome over dense text without a scroll-edge fade/dim or a uniform opaque bar; (2) don't split primary navigation into floating pills that shrink targets; (3) keep controls positionally stable (no appearing/disappearing toolbar buttons); (4) don't animate idle UI (shimmer, pulse, auto-morph); (5) ship the opaque tier as the default for data-dense surfaces, with translucency as a preference.

### Gaps
- I couldn't fetch Ars Technica's or The Verge's iOS 27 reviews. No independent performance or battery measurements of Liquid Glass were found.
