# SLA timer: how long an SLA has left, in a queue cell or a record header

Date: 2026-11-20 · Status: built in this change · Source: the enterprise-UX study, roadmap item #17
(`Docs/research/reports/UIx enterprise UX study.md`; notes `itsm_competitors.md`) · Roadmap:
`Docs/component-roadmap.md` listed `sla.css` as a planned wrapper.

## Why

Every ITSM leader sorts its queues by SLA breach time and shows a breach warning before the breach
(Jira Service Management, Freshservice, ServiceNow, Halo). Halo fires OLA warnings at up to three
thresholds (a share of the time elapsed, or the working time left) and recalculates them when the clock
pauses and resumes; its "Request info" action pauses the resolution SLA. UIx had the `sla` CSS and the
status tones but no component, so TENSOR derived the state by hand in each list and in the detail rail.

## What it is

- **`slaStatus(input, now)`**, a pure function. It reads the instants the server computed and says where
  the clock stands: the state, the time left (negative once breached), the deadline, whether the clock
  runs, and the next instant at which the state or the minute shown changes.
- **`slaSortValue(input, now)`**, the queue column's sort key: ascending puts the most overdue breach
  first, then the least time left (a paused clock by its frozen time), and met or no SLA last. The "last"
  is a finite number (`SLA_SORT_LAST`), because `Infinity − Infinity` is `NaN` in a comparator (TENSOR
  ADR-0054 hit this).
- **`<SlaTimer />`**, one line in a table cell (`variant="cell"`, the default) or three in a record
  header (`variant="header"`: the SLA's name, the time, the deadline or the pause's reason).

The server owns the clock. A business calendar, holidays and pauses are its business; the kit never
computes working time. It takes `dueAt` (as the server projected it), `startedAt` (for share thresholds),
`breachedAt` (the server's recorded breach wins over the wall clock), `paused` with `pausedAt` and
`pausedReason`, and `completedAt`.

## States and thresholds

| State | When | Cell | Colour | Mark |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `on-track` | before the first threshold | `2 hr 14 min` | ink (hushed) | none |
| `notice` | an optional first threshold | `4 hr` | ink | amber ring |
| `warning` | default: 75 % of the span, or 2 h left without a start | `1 hr 20 min` | amber text | dot |
| `urgent` | default: 90 % of the span, or 30 min left | `12 min` | attention orange, semibold | diamond |
| `breached` | at or after the deadline, or at `breachedAt` | `35 min over` | overdue red, semibold | dot |
| `paused` | `paused` | `Paused` | ink | two bars |
| `met` | `completedAt` before the deadline | `Met` | ink | none |
| `none` | no valid deadline | `—` | muted | none |

Thresholds take one of `elapsed` (a share of `startedAt` → `dueAt`), `remaining` (milliseconds before
`dueAt`) or `at` (an instant, for a threshold the server projected through its calendar). Up to three;
more warns once in development. The worst level crossed wins. `[]` shows no level before the breach.

The brief named the 90 % level "critical". It is `urgent` here: in the calm kit "critical" is red, and
red is kept for the one state that is broken now, the breach. Before the breach the time pressure takes
the kit's attention orange (the `sla-at-risk` tone), shown with a different shape.

## Words

- **Shown:** `Intl.NumberFormat` units (`2 hr 14 min`; German `1 Std., 20 Min.`) joined by
  `Intl.ListFormat`, the two largest units, floored, so a timer never shows more time than is left.
  Under a minute: `< 1 min`. `format="relative"` uses `Intl.RelativeTimeFormat` ("in 2 hours"),
  `format="absolute"` the date and time.
- **Spoken** (a visually hidden span; the visible copy and the mark are `aria-hidden`):
  "Resolution: Due in 2 hours 14 minutes, warning. Deadline 20 Nov 2026, 15:30". A breach: "Breached 35
  minutes ago. Deadline …". Paused: "Paused, 4 hours left. Waiting for the requester".
- **Hover:** the absolute deadline in `title` (and the pause's reason). A cell timer is not focusable, so
  the deadline is also in the spoken text and under the header.
- Every word is a label (`UixLabelsProvider` `slaTimer`, `states` merged per state), with `{time}`,
  `{label}`, `{text}` placeholders so a translation can reorder. The locale is the `locale` prop, else the
  provider's (`useUixLocale()`: `locale`, then `dateFormat.locale`), else the browser's. `slaTimer` is in every
  language pack (`@tensor_1/react/locales/<tag>`).

## Time

- No fixed tick. Each timer asks one shared scheduler (`sla-clock.ts`) to wake it at `nextChangeAt`: the
  next whole minute to the deadline, or a threshold sooner. Wake-ups land on whole seconds, so a queue's
  rows that change in the same second render together. A paused, met or breached-and-stopped clock never
  wakes.
- A hidden tab schedules nothing; when it shows again every timer is woken once to catch up.
- `now` fixes the clock (tests, a server render); the timer then never ticks.
- A hydrating client renders no time first and then its own, so a cached server page never shows a stale
  time or state.

## Motion and sound

Nothing pulses, loops or transitions (calm kit; `check-motion`). A state change is a re-render.
`announce` (off by default) says a crossing into a worse level once, politely, through the kit's one
polite region; never on mount, never for a change of props, never each minute. A queue of timers must not
talk; turn it on for the open record's timer only.

## Forced colours

Each mark paints `CanvasText` (a fill for the dot and the diamond, a border for the ring and the bars), so
the shapes survive; the words carry the rest.
